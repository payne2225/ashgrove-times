Millions watch Spain's first total solar eclipse since 1912
Totality ran about a minute over Spain under cloudless skies; much of Iceland was clouded over.
By Wire Reports

Millions of people across northern Spain, Portugal, Iceland and Greenland watched a total solar eclipse Wednesday evening, the first visible from mainland Spain since 1912. The moon's shadow crossed the North Atlantic and reached the Iberian Peninsula close to sunset, and cheers went up at viewing sites as the sun went out.

Totality lasted less than 2 1/2 minutes at its maximum, off Iceland's west coast, and about a minute by the time the shadow reached Spain and Portugal. Spain set up 350 official viewing areas and deployed about 33,500 officers, expecting at least a half-million extra visitors.

Skies were cloudless over Spain and Portugal, much of Iceland was overcast, and in Britain the sun was more than 90% obscured. Spain's next total eclipse falls Aug. 2, 2027.
