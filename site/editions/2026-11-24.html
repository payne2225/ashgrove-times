Nvidia reinstates Huang as CEO; Nasdaq posts its best day since April 2025
The board voted unanimously 11 days after he resigned over an affair with an alleged Chinese agent; Trump called it the 'correct and proper decision'
By Wire Reports

Nvidia's board voted unanimously Monday to bring Jensen Huang back as president, chief executive and a director 11 days after he resigned, and Wall Street followed: the Nasdaq composite closed up 6.5% and the S&P 500 about 4.7%, the best day for both since April 2025, Euronews reported. Nvidia shares rose about 31.5% from Friday's close.

Huang stepped down after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who was alleged to be a spy; Attorney General Todd Blanche said Nov. 12 that the FBI was investigating reports she had worked for the Chinese government, and Beijing rejected the allegation. Board chair Harvey Jones said directors were satisfied with Huang's "candor and ability to lead" after a series of meetings and had input from senior U.S. government officials; a week earlier the board had said it did not intend to reinstate him.

President Donald Trump called the reversal the "correct and proper decision" and said the board "acted really responsibly"; NBC News reported that Trump, Vice President JD Vance and Blanche worked through the weekend to restore Huang's position. An independent committee is still investigating whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, a second will oversee trade compliance, and Huang is due before three House committees Dec. 1 for a transcribed interview that will not be public. Colette Kress, the interim chief executive, stays on as chief financial officer.
