Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan sign mutual defense pact in Mecca
The Makkah agreement treats an attack on one as an attack on all.
By Wire Reports

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed a trilateral defense treaty in Mecca on Friday, committing each state to treat an armed attack on any one of them as an attack on all three.

The joint statement says the accord is meant to strengthen collective deterrence and develop all aspects of defense cooperation. Saudi deputy minister Rayed Krimly said it is not an effort to build a military axis or a sectarian bloc, and is not tied to nuclear ambitions.

Saudi territory has been struck by Iran and Iran-backed forces in Yemen and Iraq since the United States and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28. Turkey fields NATO's second-largest military; Pakistan is the only Muslim-majority state with nuclear arms.
