Iran hits U.S. bases in Kuwait and UAE as Trump vows more strikes
Iran puts its toll from this week's U.S. strikes at 18 dead; Tehran says it laid new mines south of Hormuz.
By Wire Reports

Iran's army said Thursday it struck U.S. communications systems, depots and aircraft hangars at Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait and radar at Al Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates with missiles and drones. Kuwait's army said its air defenses were engaging the attack, and its foreign ministry called it a flagrant violation of sovereignty. Neither country reported casualties.

Iran's health minister put the toll from this week's U.S. strikes at 18 dead and at least 108 wounded, and the Iranian Red Crescent asked the International Criminal Court to investigate the strike on a wedding in Kuhestak. Central Command said it does not target civilians and is looking into the reports; it said Wednesday's strikes hit air defenses, radar and mine-laying capability.

Trump called it "a very heavy attack" and said the U.S. is prepared to strike again "anytime we want"; Secretary of State Rubio said the June memorandum with Iran has essentially ended. An Iranian military spokesman said more mines were laid south of the Strait of Hormuz overnight, waters Central Command had declared cleared. Brent traded near $95.
