Trump calls Iran war 'effectively over' but keeps the blockade in place
After meeting Qatar's prime minister, he said more strikes don't seem necessary and the U.S. is 'locked and loaded'
By Wire Reports

President Donald Trump said Monday that the war with Iran is "effectively over" militarily, speaking at an unannounced White House briefing after a closed-door meeting with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, who brought a two-day Iranian proposal to end the conflict. Vice President JD Vance sat in on the meeting.

"Taking military action doesn't seem to be a very necessary thing, but we're locked and loaded, ready to go for any reason that changes," Trump said. He said the United States would keep "the status quo" at the request of Iran's neighbors: the naval blockade of Iranian ports and the new Task Force Iron Gate, meant to cut trade by land and across the Caspian Sea. The conflict would end "fairly soon," he said.

Tehran rejected the framing. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, called the remarks a distraction and told Washington to stop "manufacturing FAKE NEWS"; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said attacks and sanctions will not end the war and the two-day plan's conditions "are still on the table." Qatar's prime minister said the talks covered "ways to progress the US-Iran talks."
