Supreme Court blocks Postal Service mail-ballot rules for midterms
Alito and Thomas dissent; Kavanaugh says election officials lack time to comply before November
By Wire Reports

The Supreme Court on Monday, in an unsigned order, refused to let the U.S. Postal Service enforce new mail-ballot regulations before the November midterm elections, leaving in place a lower-court order that blocked rules stemming from a March executive order by President Donald Trump.

The government "is unlikely to succeed on the merits," the unsigned order said. Justices Samuel Alito and Clarence Thomas dissented. Justice Brett Kavanaugh wrote separately that the Postal Service arguably had authority to issue the rule but that state and local election officials did not have time to implement it before the elections.

The rule would have required states to submit lists of eligible voters to a Postal Service portal and use approved envelope designs, with noncompliant ballots subject to rejection. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani blocked it in late August, and a second federal judge in Washington did the same Sunday in a suit brought by the Democratic National Committee.
