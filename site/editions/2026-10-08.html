Isaias, season's first hurricane, bears down on the Gulf Coast
Hurricane warning runs from Ocean Springs, Miss., to Florida's Bay-Gulf county line; two states declare emergencies
By Wire Reports

Isaias strengthened into the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season late Wednesday and is forecast to come ashore between the Mississippi coast and the Florida Panhandle late Friday or early Saturday, the National Hurricane Center said, with a hurricane warning posted from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to Florida's Bay-Gulf county line.

At 5 a.m. ET Thursday the center put Isaias about 420 miles south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi River with top sustained winds of 80 mph, moving east-northeast at 9 mph and still strengthening. It forecast a peak surge of 5 to 7 feet from Ocean Springs to Indian Pass, Florida, and in Mobile Bay, and 3 to 7 inches of rain, locally 10, along the coast.

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis declared a state of emergency for 25 counties and Alabama Gov. Kay Ivey for 40; Gulf Shores and Orange Beach, Alabama, urged voluntary evacuations, NBC News reported. Isaias formed more than four months into the season, nearly tying the 1905 record for the latest first Atlantic hurricane, a lull meteorologists tie to a strong El Nino, CBS News said.
