Houthis seize Yemen's Red Sea coast as Saudis shut oil pipeline
Crude tops $100 a barrel after rebels take the Bab al-Mandeb islands; Riyadh says drones came from Iraq
By Wire Reports

Houthi fighters completed their takeover of Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline and the Bab al-Mandeb strait's islands Friday, while Saudi Arabia suspended its cross-country East-West crude pipeline following drone attacks, pushing international oil prices above $100 a barrel.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the operation expelled Saudi-backed forces from six districts covering 5,400 square kilometers and declared that "maritime navigation is safe for all companies except for Saudi vessels." The Saudi Energy Ministry said the pipeline, the kingdom's main export route avoiding the Strait of Hormuz, was suspended as a "precautionary measure."

About 12% of the world's goods typically move through Bab al-Mandeb, AP reported. Iraq said Saturday its prime minister had dismissed the military commander in Maysan province after finding the attacks were launched from there, and Iran said it will meet Gulf states in Oman on Monday on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. U.S. diesel passed $6 a gallon Friday.
