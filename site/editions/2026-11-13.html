Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia's chief executive after 33 years
Finance chief Colette Kress takes over on an interim basis; shares closed down 4.3% and House chairs gave Huang until Friday evening to agree to testify
By Wire Reports

Nvidia's board said Thursday that Jensen Huang, 63, the co-founder who has run the chipmaker since 1993, resigned as president, chief executive and director effective immediately, four days after he told employees he had an affair with a former executive of a Singapore data-center firm whom U.S. intelligence agencies believe was a Chinese asset, an allegation Beijing denies.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress becomes interim chief executive while keeping her post, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first independent chair. Shares fell as much as 14.3% at the open and closed down 4.3% at $166.19; CBS News put the market value erased since last week's close at almost $500 billion, and the stock was 27% off its record through Wednesday.

The chairs of the House China, Foreign Affairs and Intelligence committees gave Huang until Friday evening to accept a Dec. 1 closed-door interview or face a subpoena, and Senate Banking chair Tim Scott wants him and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick before his panel in December. Independent directors have retained former acting U.S. Attorney Joon H. Kim to investigate; quarterly results are due Tuesday.
