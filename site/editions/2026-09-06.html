U.S. hits three Iranian tankers after missile attack on Navy warships
Iran's Guard says it struck three U.S.-linked ships and an unmanned vessel in the strait; no U.S. injuries reported
By Wire Reports

The U.S. military said Saturday it destroyed one Iranian crude oil tanker and permanently disabled two others after Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at a U.S. aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer, and Iran said early Sunday its forces had attacked an unmanned U.S. vessel trying to enter the Strait of Hormuz.

U.S. Central Command said the M/T Downy off Kharg Island and the M/T Stark 1 near Jask were disabled and the M/T Kylo, which was not carrying oil, was destroyed in the Gulf of Oman. It said both Navy ships evaded the missiles and no U.S. personnel were hurt. Adm. Brad Cooper said, "If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost."

The Guard said it targeted three tankers using an unauthorized route and three U.S.-affiliated vessels, and Iran's Foreign Ministry called the strikes illegal and aggressive. The war began Feb. 28 and a ceasefire was announced in June, but fighting resumed last week; Brent crude was above $95 a barrel late in the week.
