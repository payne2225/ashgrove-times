U.S. imposes 50% tariffs on Canada as talks collapse at deadline
Carney suspended negotiations and said Canada will match the duties dollar for dollar.
By Wire Reports

Trade talks between the United States and Canada collapsed Friday night, minutes before a midnight deadline, and 50% tariffs on about $20 billion in Canadian goods took effect at midnight. The duties fall on hockey sticks, building materials, liquors and some clothing.

Prime Minister Mark Carney sent his negotiators back to Ottawa, saying late changes to the U.S. terms were unfair and uneconomic. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Canada declined to finalize a deal reached earlier in the week, and that Washington had offered cuts on steel, aluminum, autos and lumber. Provincial bans on American alcohol were among the sticking points.
