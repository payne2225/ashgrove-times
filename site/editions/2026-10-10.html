Isaias comes ashore at Destin at 105 mph, cuts power to 834,000
Storm fell to a post-tropical cyclone over Alabama by 4 a.m.; NHC hands off advisories as flood threat moves north
By Wire Reports

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Fla., at about 8:30 p.m. CDT Friday with maximum sustained winds of 105 mph, the National Hurricane Center said. It came ashore as a Category 2 storm after briefly reaching major-hurricane strength in the Gulf on Friday afternoon, the first hurricane to hit the eastern United States since Milton in October 2024.

A station near Santa Rosa Sound recorded a 116 mph gust and Pensacola City Hall 102 mph, the center said, and a NOAA gauge at Panama City Beach measured water 5.6 feet above the normal high-tide line. By 5 a.m. ET Saturday, poweroutage.us counted 834,061 customers dark in Florida, Alabama and Georgia, CBS News reported. No deaths had been confirmed.

The center issued its last advisory at 4 a.m. CDT with Isaias a post-tropical cyclone 25 miles from Montgomery, Ala., carrying 35 mph winds north toward the Tennessee Valley. It forecast 4 to 8 inches of rain, locally 15, over southern Alabama, the Panhandle and southwest Georgia, with a tornado risk into the Carolinas.
