Israel's election ends with no majority as Netanyahu bloc leads count
With 72% counted, Netanyahu's bloc holds 54 seats and Eisenkot's 48; final results are due Friday
By Wire Reports

Israel's election produced no governing majority, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc leading Wednesday's partial count but short of the 61 Knesset seats needed to govern, and the opposition bloc of former military chief Gadi Eisenkot also falling short.

With about 72% of ballots counted by the Central Elections Committee, Netanyahu's bloc held 54 seats, Eisenkot's alliance 48 and the Arab parties 18, Al Jazeera reported. Official data cited by CBS News at the 60% mark gave Likud 21.4% of the vote and Eisenkot's Yashar party 16.2%. Turnout reached 77.7%, the highest since 1999.

Netanyahu called for a "national right-wing government" and ruled out a coalition with Arab parties; Eisenkot said voters had chosen change. Final results are due by Friday, after which President Isaac Herzog nominates a candidate who gets up to 42 days to form a government. If none forms, Israel faces its sixth election since 2019.
