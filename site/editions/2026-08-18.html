U.S.-Iran truce memorandum expires with no talks under way
Neither side proposed an extension, and the Strait of Hormuz stays closed.
By Wire Reports

The 60-day deadline the United States and Iran set for a permanent peace deal expired Monday with no negotiations under way, leaving the Strait of Hormuz shut and each government blaming the other.

The 14-point memorandum signed June 17 traded a ceasefire, the lifting of the U.S. naval blockade and $300B in reconstruction for Iran clearing mines from the strait. It broke down within weeks over which shipping route Iran would accept, and President Donald Trump declared it over on July 7.

Iran wants the blockade lifted, U.S. forces withdrawn and war reparations; Trump has rejected all three. Pakistan's foreign ministry said it is still working to bring both sides to a table.
