Justice Department charges Comey a third time over 2016 leak
Prosecutors filed a sealed criminal information to stop a 10-year clock; his lawyers call it time-barred
By Wire Reports

The Justice Department has charged former FBI Director James Comey with leaking classified information to a newspaper in October 2016, court records unsealed Monday in Washington show, the third criminal case against him since President Trump returned to office.

Prosecutors filed a two-count criminal information under seal on Oct. 5, alleging Comey transmitted national defense information when he directed staff to speak with a New York Times reporter. The department said the filing tolls the 10-year statute of limitations; NBC News reported Comey has not been indicted and cannot be tried without one unless he waives it.

Comey's attorney, Patrick Fitzgerald, said the charges are time-barred and that two earlier investigations ended without charges; Comey said in a video he is "still innocent." The first case against him was dismissed and a second, over a seashell photo, is pending. CBS News reported the government agreed to wait 30 days before seeking an indictment.
