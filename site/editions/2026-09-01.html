Jury convicts Duane Davis of first-degree murder in Tupac Shakur's killing
Deliberations took less than three hours; Davis, 63, is the only person ever charged in the 30-year-old case.
By Wire Reports

AClark County jury on Monday found Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, guilty of first-degree murder with a deadly weapon in the 1996 drive-by killing of Tupac Shakur, deliberating less than three hours after a nine-day Las Vegas trial. Davis is the only person ever charged in a shooting that went unresolved for nearly 30 years.

Shakur, 25, was stopped at a red light near the Strip on Sept. 7, 1996, when a white Cadillac pulled alongside and shots were fired; he died six days later. Prosecutors built the case on Davis's own repeated public accounts that he rode in the Cadillac and handed a gun into the back seat, calling the shooting retaliation for a beating of his nephew by Shakur's entourage earlier that evening.

The jury heard 24 prosecution witnesses against three for the defense, which argued no footage or records placed Davis in Las Vegas that night. Sentencing is set for October, NPR reported, and Davis faces up to life in prison; CBS reported Shakur's sister wept in the courtroom as the verdict was read.
