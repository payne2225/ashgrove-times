Cut fiber line halts flights at 5 Northeast airports for hours
An NJ Transit crew severed the FAA's backup cable after a primary circuit failed; Newark saw no landings for nearly 8 hours
By Wire Reports

The Federal Aviation Administration halted flights into Newark, Teterboro, Philadelphia, LaGuardia and Kennedy airports for much of Monday after a data circuit at its Philadelphia approach control facility failed and a construction crew in New Jersey cut the backup fiber-optic line, on the day world leaders arrived in New York for the U.N. General Assembly.

Ground stops began about 10 a.m. at Newark, Teterboro and Philadelphia and were extended to Kennedy and LaGuardia about 1 p.m., NBC New York reported. Flightradar24 data showed no commercial flights landing at Newark from about 9:40 a.m. until after 5:20 p.m., and NBC News reported, citing FlightAware, that 619 flights at Newark and 197 at Philadelphia had been canceled by 6 p.m.

NJ Transit said its crew on the Delco Lead project severed the cable about 9:45 a.m. while working about 10 feet from utility markings it said may have been inaccurate; Verizon said it bore no responsibility. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Monday evening the lines were repaired, and CBS New York reported hundreds of flights still canceled or delayed at 11 p.m.
