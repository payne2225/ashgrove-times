U.S. and Iran trade fire over the Strait of Hormuz
CENTCOM hit launchers it says were set to mine the strait; Jordan intercepted eight missiles fired back at bases hosting U.S. forces.
By Wire Reports

American forces struck two Revolutionary Guard rocket launchers on Iran's Larak Island on Sunday, and Iran answered before dawn Monday with ballistic missiles aimed at U.S. forces at the King Hussein and Al-Azraq air bases in Jordan - the first direct exchange of fire between the two countries in a month.

U.S. Central Command called the strike a 'limited, precise action' to keep Guard forces from laying sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, where American ships had been clearing shipping lanes; Iran's Fars news agency reported soldiers killed and wounded on the island without giving figures. The Guard named its reply 'Punishment of the Aggressor' and said it also downed a U.S. MQ-9 drone over the strait.

Jordan's military said it intercepted eight missiles that crossed into its airspace at dawn, and no injuries were reported at either base. President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran was 'not looking for war' but would 'never sit still in the face of any aggression.' The strait ordinarily carries about a fifth of the world's oil.
