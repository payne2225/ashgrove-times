Latvia's Kulbergs wins record 42 seats in low-turnout election
United List takes about 35% of Saturday's vote, the most for one party since independence; turnout falls to 51.8%, a record low
By Wire Reports

Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs' centrist United List won Saturday's Latvian parliamentary election with about 35% of the vote and 42 of the Saeima's 100 seats, the most any single party has taken since the country regained independence in 1991, public broadcaster LSM reported Sunday with 99.9% of precincts counted.

Latvia First took 17 seats, Sovereign Power 15, the National Alliance 10, the Progressives nine and New Unity seven, LSM said. Kulbergs needs 51 votes for a majority, and LSM sketched a 59-seat coalition with the National Alliance and New Unity. Turnout was 51.8%, down from 59.4% in 2022 and the lowest on record, NBC News reported.

Kulbergs, 47, took office in May after the previous coalition collapsed over its handling of Ukrainian drone incidents, and said Saturday night he would not cooperate with Latvia First, DW reported. Al Jazeera said the result is not expected to change Latvia's pro-European course; President Edgars Rinkevics nominates a prime minister after coalition talks, typically about a week.
