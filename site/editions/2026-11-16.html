ULA's Vulcan rocket fails, losing 40 Amazon internet satellites
The Centaur upper stage faltered 10 minutes after Sunday's Cape Canaveral liftoff, the first total loss for ULA since it formed in 2006
By Wire Reports

AUnited Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket carrying 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites failed about 10 minutes after lifting off at 12:40 p.m. ET Sunday from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Fla. ULA said the vehicle "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit," the first outright failure of a ULA rocket since the Boeing-Lockheed Martin venture formed in 2006.

Telemetry on ULA's webcast showed the six solid boosters separating on time, the two BE-4 first-stage engines burning past the published timeline and the Centaur upper stage making unusual attitude adjustments early in its burn, CBS News reported. ULA has not given a cause; it said it will find one, take corrective action and return to flight "as quickly as possible." Amazon called ULA "a trusted partner."

The flight was Vulcan's fifth and its first in nine months, after solid-booster nozzle problems on two earlier missions led the Space Force to pause national-security launches. It was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon bought for a constellation that needs more than 3,300 satellites; 396 had launched before Sunday, Spaceflight Now reported, and Amazon also holds launches on New Glenn, Ariane 6 and Falcon 9.
