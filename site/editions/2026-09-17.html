Fed raises rates a quarter point, first hike since 2023
Unanimous 12-0 vote lifts the target range to 3.75%-4%; Warsh says inflation 'too high and has been for too long'
By Wire Reports

The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point Wednesday, to a range of 3.75% to 4%, its first increase since 2023 and a break with President Donald Trump's repeated demands for cuts. The Federal Open Market Committee approved the move on a 12-0 vote, the Fed said in its statement.

"Inflation remains elevated," the committee said, adding that the action "will support a timelier return" to its 2% goal. Chair Kevin Warsh told reporters that "the plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long." Annual inflation ran 3.4% in August, NPR reported, with gasoline more than a third of the monthly rise as the Iran war lifts fuel prices.

Projections released with the decision show policymakers expect on average one more quarter-point increase this year and none in 2027, NPR reported. Trump posted that rates "should be 1% or less," then told reporters he still has confidence in Warsh, who took over in May, Al Jazeera reported. The Dow fell 630 points on the day, NBC News said.
