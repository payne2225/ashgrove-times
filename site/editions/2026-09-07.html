Amazon cargo jet overruns Miami runway, hits vehicles; 5 dead
The Boeing 767 freighter from San Juan left the runway at about 128 mph and burned; the NTSB briefs Monday
By Wire Reports

Prime Air Flight 7598, a Boeing 767-300 freighter operated for Amazon by 21 Air, ran off Runway 30 at Miami International Airport after landing from San Juan, Puerto Rico, at about 2 p.m. Sunday, struck vehicles on a road beside the airfield and burned, killing five people and injuring five, officials said.

ABC News reported the jet left the runway at about 128 mph and came to rest on its side in a field with a fuel leak and, Fire Chief Raied Jadallah said, a "very complex fire" in the engines. About 200 fire-rescue personnel responded; three of the injured were critical. NBC News said the two pilots were among the dead, which CBS Miami said officials had not confirmed.

The 32-year-old aircraft was delivered as a passenger jet in 1994 and converted to cargo in 2015. The airport halted arrivals for about three hours and reopened two of four runways by 5 p.m.; about 250 flights were delayed. The NTSB sent a go-team led by Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy, who is to brief reporters Monday. Amazon said it was "heartbroken."
