Nvidia posts record $115.8B quarter as board rules out Huang's return
Shares rose about 4% after hours; interim CEO Colette Kress said demand remains well above supply
By Wire Reports

Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion on Tuesday in its first earnings report since founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, and board chair Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO" hours after Huang offered to return if directors asked him.

Revenue more than doubled from $57 billion a year earlier and topped the roughly $109 billion analysts expected, according to FactSet, CBS News reported. Adjusted earnings were $2.68 a share, and the company forecast $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter. Shares rose about 4% in after-hours trading after closing down 1.7% at $153.51.

Kress said the company would study new Commerce Department rules limiting advanced chip sales abroad but did not expect them to materially affect sales. The board has hired Heidrick & Struggles to search for a permanent chief. Huang is scheduled to sit for an interview with three House committees on Dec. 1 over Nvidia's chip sales to China.
