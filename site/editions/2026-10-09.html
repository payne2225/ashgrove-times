Isaias strengthens to 110 mph ahead of Gulf Coast landfall tonight
Mandatory evacuations in two Florida counties and three Alabama beach towns; surge of 5 to 7 feet forecast from Dauphin Island to Indian Pass
By Wire Reports

Hurricane Isaias strengthened to 110 mph early Friday, just short of major-hurricane strength, and was moving north-northeast at 15 mph about 200 miles south-southeast of the mouth of the Mississippi River, the National Hurricane Center said at 4 a.m. CDT. It is forecast to come ashore between Ocean Springs, Miss., and Florida's Bay County tonight or early Saturday.

The center said Isaias is likely to become a major hurricane this morning, with slight weakening before landfall. It forecast a peak surge of 5 to 7 feet from Dauphin Island, Ala., to Indian Pass, Fla., and 4 to 8 inches of rain, locally 15, across southern Alabama, the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, with up to 10 inches possible in the Southern Appalachians.

Florida ordered mandatory evacuations in parts of Okaloosa and Walton counties, Alabama ordered non-residents out of Gulf Shores, Orange Beach and Dauphin Island, and Florida, Alabama and Georgia declared emergencies, CBS News and NPR reported. Nearly two-thirds of Gulf oil production had been shut in by noon Thursday, NBC News reported, citing federal regulators.
