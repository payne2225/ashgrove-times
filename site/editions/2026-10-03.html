G7 agrees to release 100 million barrels of oil to curb diesel prices
Four-month drawdown through the International Energy Agency begins at once, with a diesel release front-loaded into the first 20 days
By Wire Reports

The Group of Seven agreed Friday to release 100 million barrels of oil through the International Energy Agency over four months, beginning immediately and front-loading a diesel release into the first 20 days, after an emergency video conference called by French President Emmanuel Macron, the group's chair.

The G7 statement said members would also refrain from energy export restrictions among themselves and coordinate refinery maintenance so plants do not shut at once, CBS News and Euronews reported. The call followed an overnight Trump-Macron conversation; Trump had threatened a U.S. diesel export ban and said afterward that "what Europe did was a great thing." Spread evenly, the release is about 830,000 barrels a day, Euronews said.

U.S. diesel averaged $6.37 a gallon Friday, up about 70% since late February, and Brent crude closed at $102.25, NBC News reported; the record diesel price was $6.52 on Sept. 22. Andy Lipow of Lipow Oil Associates told NBC a release of this size could cut diesel by about 25 cents a gallon temporarily. The IEA released 400 million barrels in March, about two-thirds of it delivered, Al Jazeera said.
