Dolly Parton, country music's great ambassador, dies at 80
The singer, songwriter and philanthropist died in Nashville after a brief battle with cancer, her representatives said
By Wire Reports

Dolly Parton, whose seven-decade career carried her from a cabin in the East Tennessee hills to the head of an entertainment and philanthropic empire, died peacefully in Nashville on Tuesday at age 80. Her representatives said she had waged a brief battle with cancer, and her nephew confirmed the death.

Parton wrote her first No. 1 hit, "Joshua," in 1970 and topped the pop chart with the theme to "9 to 5," stacking up dozens of top-10 country records on the way to a 2022 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction. Her Dollywood theme park anchors the Smoky Mountains economy, and her Imagination Library mails a free book a month to children from birth to age 5.

Her philanthropy included a $1 million gift to Vanderbilt University Medical Center that helped fund Moderna's COVID-19 vaccine, and a Knoxville children's hospital was renamed in her honor. Her husband of nearly 60 years, Carl Dean, died in 2025. Tributes poured in Tuesday night from across music and public life.
