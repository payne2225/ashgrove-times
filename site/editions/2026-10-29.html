Spain's rail toll reaches 68 as minister blames restart order
Puente says controllers restarted the Renfe train with no speed limit after a signal failure; three days of mourning begin
By Wire Reports

ARenfe high-speed train from Madrid to Seville struck the rear of a stopped Iryo train near Urda in Toledo province on Wednesday morning, killing at least 68 people, the regional government of Castilla-La Mancha said, in Spain's second deadly high-speed collision in nine months.

Transport Minister Oscar Puente said a computer failure knocked out the safety system on a 63-kilometer stretch at 8:30 a.m. and halted every train; the control center then told the Renfe train, carrying 397 people, to restart without speed restrictions while a stop signal was still active, and it hit the Iryo train, carrying 147, at 8:53, AP and CBS News reported. Puente called the order "inexplicable."

The government declared three days of mourning through Saturday, and Adif suspended high-speed service between Madrid and Andalusia. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, who faces an election Nov. 29, called two such crashes in a year "unacceptable"; a January collision on the same line at Adamuz killed 46, Al Jazeera reported. A minute's silence at Madrid's Cibeles Square turned into a protest calling for Puente to resign.
