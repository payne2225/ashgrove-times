Thanksgiving storm knocks out power to 940,000 and floods both coasts
Central Park logs its wettest Thanksgiving on record at 1.82 inches; 462 flights canceled, 2,600 delayed
By Wire Reports

Twin storm systems hammered the East and West coasts on Thanksgiving, flooding shore towns from North Carolina to New Jersey and cutting power to about 940,000 homes and businesses at the peak. Almost 700,000 customers were still dark early Friday, PowerOutage.us said.

Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Maryland each had more than 130,000 customers out Thursday night. FlightAware counted 462 cancellations and 2,600 delays. Central Park recorded 1.82 inches of rain, its wettest Thanksgiving on record, the National Weather Service said, and LaGuardia clocked a 56 mph gust. St. Louis got 7.3 inches of snow; Ste. Genevieve, Mo., 14.

New Jersey and parts of New York remain under states of emergency. Wind closed the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel for hours, surf shut NC 12 to Hatteras Island, and 1.9 million Maryland water customers were asked to conserve after outages hit filtration plants. Pacific tide flooding runs into Friday, the weather service said; the Macy's parade steps off Saturday.
