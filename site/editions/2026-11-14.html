FAA grounds every U.S. departure for four hours, blames aging routers
More than 4,000 flights were canceled; Duffy says old switches overloaded and Mullin says there was no cyberattack
By Wire Reports

The Federal Aviation Administration halted every departing flight in the United States for more than four hours Friday after a telecommunications failure hit its en-route air traffic control centers, the first nationwide ground stop since January 2023. The order ran from 3:54 p.m. to 8:15 p.m. ET, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

FlightAware listed more than 10,000 delays and over 4,000 cancellations Friday, and Flightradar24 counted 700 more flights scrubbed for Saturday. Duffy said the network's old switches and routers "got overloaded with data" and fed controllers inconsistent information. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no cyberattack.

Sources told CBS News an update to the FAA telecommunications network run by L3Harris caused a reboot and crash; the company did not respond. Duffy said he expects normal operations by the Thanksgiving rush and renewed a call for Congress to fund a full overhaul, which Mullin put at about $50 billion. Sen. Tammy Duckworth called for a full investigation.
