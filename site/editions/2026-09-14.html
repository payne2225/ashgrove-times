Sweden's center-left leads by 3 seats as the far right slips
Sweden Democrats lose ground for the first time; late ballots are counted from Wednesday and a final result is due by the end of the week
By Wire Reports

Sweden's center-left opposition held a three-seat lead over Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's governing bloc early Monday with 94% of votes counted, the Swedish Election Authority said, putting Social Democrat leader Magdalena Andersson in position to form a government after Sunday's parliamentary election.

The opposition was on course for 176 seats in the 349-member Riksdag to 173 for the governing bloc, the authority's preliminary count showed. The Social Democrats led with 28.1% of the vote, down 2.2 points from 2022 and the party's weakest result in more than a century. The far-right Sweden Democrats fell to third, behind the Moderates, the first time the party's share has declined.

Counting of late postal and overseas ballots begins Wednesday, and a final result is expected by the end of the week. Kristersson said he would contact other party leaders once the result is final and that "it's a good idea to look at possible combinations." Andersson said Sweden "will get a new government" if the numbers hold.
