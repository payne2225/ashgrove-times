Johnson steps aside as House GOP leader after midterm losses
Scalise and Jordan seek the post before a Nov. 10 vote; Trump weighs Hegseth's future, sources say
By Wire Reports

House Speaker Mike Johnson told staff on a call Wednesday that he will not run for Republican leader in the new Congress, a day after Democrats won the House and Senate, a source familiar with his plans told CBS News and NPR. Johnson, R-La., had said in October he intended to stay on as leader if Republicans lost the majority.

House Majority Leader Steve Scalise sent colleagues a five-page letter Wednesday saying his "number one goal as Republican Leader will be to win back the majority," and Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan wrote a two-page letter calling himself "battle-tested on the oversight front," CBS News reported. House Republicans hold leadership elections Nov. 10, a GOP aide said.

The losses reached the Pentagon as well. Trump has asked advisers whether Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth should stay, CBS News reported, citing multiple sources, and NBC News reported, citing two people, that Trump told allies he is weighing replacements. Trump said Hegseth is "doing a very good job"; Vice President JD Vance wrote on X, "The reason we lost is the war."
