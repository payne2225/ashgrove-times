Iran sets six conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz
Tehran's terms reach past the June memorandum even as an Oman corridor deal nears
By Wire Reports

Iran's Supreme National Security Council said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the United States "corrects its behavior," setting out six conditions in a statement carried by Iranian state media on Saturday.

Council secretary Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, a Revolutionary Guard commander, demanded an end to U.S. threats, a permanent halt to attacks on Iran and its allies, a lifting of the naval blockade, compensation for two wars, an end to sanctions and the release of frozen assets.

The list goes beyond the June 17 memorandum, recasting phased steps as preconditions. Iran and Oman have nearly settled a corridor plan routing inbound traffic through Iranian waters and outbound through Omani waters for 60 days. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called it very close.
