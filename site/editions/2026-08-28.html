Nepal flood toll passes 470 as a barrier lake threatens new deluge
More than 1,500 are missing across Nepal and Tibet, and a blocked river is swelling above the disaster zone
By Wire Reports

Nepal's prime minister's office said Friday that 469 people are confirmed dead and 977 missing, two days after a glacier collapse sent a wall of ice, rock and mud down Himalayan river valleys and buried whole villages. Chinese authorities count three dead and 558 missing across the border in Tibet, where 260 of the missing are foreign nationals.

Attention is now on an unstable barrier lake at the Nepal-China border, formed where flood debris dammed a river. Chinese authorities say it holds about 2 million cubic meters of water, with 3 million more expected within three days; a burst would send a second flood down valleys where roughly 42 kilometers of road are already gone. About 1,550 people have been rescued, 800 of them by helicopter, and Nepal has deployed 30,000 security personnel.

Nepal says 644 of its missing are foreigners, and NPR reported about 90 Americans remain unaccounted for, with no American deaths confirmed by the State Department as of Thursday. Kathmandu says it does not need international search teams; the U.S. has pledged $500,000 and the U.N. $2.5 million, and India has sent helicopters with supplies.
