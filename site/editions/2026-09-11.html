Houthis take Mocha and a strait island, closing their grip on Bab al-Mandeb
Boats carrying fighters reached Mayyun Island on Friday; the U.N. envoy warns of full-scale war and Brent holds above $100
By Wire Reports

Yemen's Houthi rebels seized the Red Sea port city of Mocha on Thursday after government forces withdrew, Yemeni officials and Houthi media said, giving the Iran-backed group control of nearly all of Yemen's Red Sea coast and a position about 80 kilometers (50 miles) from the Bab al-Mandeb strait.

Tariq Saleh of Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council called the retreat a "tactical withdrawal" to regroup. France 24 reported the coastal fighting since last week has killed more than 500 people, most of them combatants, and displaced nearly 20,000. The Houthi-run Saba agency said Saudi jets flew 40 more strikes Thursday on Taiz, Hodeidah, al-Jawf and Marib.

By Friday morning boats carrying Houthi fighters had reached Mayyun Island, in the middle of the strait, after government forces withdrew, a local official told AFP. The U.N. Security Council met in emergency session Thursday; envoy Hans Grundberg said Yemen faces its gravest risk of full-scale war since the 2022 truce, and crude is trading above $100 a barrel.
