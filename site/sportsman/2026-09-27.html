Sheepshead take a fiddler crab tight to the pilings on either moving tide, and nothing wants the slack at 9:16, 3:55 or 9:34. The water at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, is 72.5, up seven-tenths from Saturday's 71.8 at the same station after Saturday's northwest wind went around to the west; Wilmington's surf forecast calls the beach water in the mid 70s. The Fisherman's Post September report, published Sept. 1 and 26 days old with nothing newer on the feed this morning, is the month's pattern, not the day's water: Native Son on a steady drum bite at docks and creek mouths, topwater early and natural bait as the sun climbed; Spring Tide on over-slot schools of 28 to 32 inches that do not stay put and on drum in the river that came in from the ocean; Speckled Specialist on reds in small groups on oysters, docks, grass points and ledges; Carolina Flats on mullet thick inshore; the Jolly Roger and Surf City piers on black drum, Virginia mullet, pompano and croakers on the bottom, with Spanish and bluefish on plugs early and late. Coastal Angler's Topsail column, also Sept. 1 and 26 days old, is the same month. Flounder remain closed and unlawful to possess on the Marine Fisheries limits page opened this morning, and the proclamation behind it runs past Sept. 30. Beach: no Rip Current Statement on Coastal Pender this morning, after Saturday's high-risk statement, and Wilmington's surf zone forecast for Surf City and Topsail Beach, issued 3:09 a.m., has a low rip current risk, surf around 2 feet on a west wind of 5 to 10 mph, sunny with highs in the upper 70s, then 1 foot Monday and Tuesday on west winds around 5, 1 to 2 feet Wednesday on an east wind and 1 to 3 feet Thursday on a southeast wind, low risk throughout. That is the calm-morning, bait-tight-to-the-beach setup the pier reports described for Spanish and bluefish on Gotcha plugs, and it holds into the week.
