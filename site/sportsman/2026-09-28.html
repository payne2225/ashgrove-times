The flood from 4:45 to the 10:20 p.m. high puts drum back onto the grass edges and dock flats through the last of the light and the fetcher's read has the moving water either side of 10:20 as the evening; the high itself comes well after dark under a 96% moon, and the ebb after it is the dock-light pattern, a small paddletail or a shrimp imitation on a light jighead drifted through the edge of the light for trout and the shrimp they are on. Sheepshead take a fiddler crab tight to the pilings on either moving tide, and nothing wants the slack at 4:04, 10:02, 4:45 or 10:20. The water at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, is 72.7, up two-tenths from Sunday's 72.5 at the same station; Wilmington's surf forecast calls the beach water in the mid 70s. The Fisherman's Post September report, published Sept. 1 and 27 days old with nothing newer on the feed this morning, is the month's pattern, not the day's water: Native Son on a steady drum bite at docks and creek mouths, topwater early and natural bait as the sun climbed; Spring Tide on over-slot schools of 28 to 32 inches that do not stay put and on drum in the river that came in from the ocean; Speckled Specialist on reds in small groups on oysters, docks, grass points and ledges; Carolina Flats on mullet thick inshore; the Jolly Roger and Surf City piers on black drum, Virginia mullet, pompano and croakers on the bottom, with Spanish and bluefish on plugs early and late. Coastal Angler's Topsail column, also Sept. 1 and 27 days old, is the same month, black drum on cut shrimp at bridges, docks and deep oyster rocks and slot reds on gold spoons and topwater. Flounder remain closed and unlawful to possess on the Marine Fisheries limits page opened this morning, and the proclamation behind it runs past Sept. 30.
