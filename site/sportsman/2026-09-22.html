The Fisherman's Post September report, published Sept. 1 and 21 days old today with nothing newer on the feed, had Native Son on a steady drum bite at docks and creek mouths on topwater early, slowing the presentation and going to natural bait as the sun rose; Speckled Specialist finding reds in small groups on oysters, docks, grass points and ledges; Spring Tide reporting over-slot schools of 28 to 32 inches that do not hold in one place, fish that are over the maximum and go back; and Carolina Flats calling the inshore drum a morning topwater bite.   • Williams River — 582.0 cfs, 3.08 feet at 5:15 a.m., flagged rising against 97.3 cfs and 1.62 feet in the fetcher's comparison. 582 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. Blown out and still coming down. The USGS two-day trace, pulled at 5:42 a.m., has the crest at 4 p.m. Sunday, 1,560 cfs and 4.52 feet, and the river has been receding since: 750 cfs and 3.39 feet at 5:15 a.m. Monday, 732 at noon, 676 at 4 p.m., 654 at 8 p.m., then a hold between 654 and 665 from 8 p.m. to 1 a.m. while showers moved through, and the fall resumed at 1:30 a.m., 644 at 2, 623 at 3, 602 at 4, 592 at 5 and 582 at 5:15. That is down 168 cfs and 0.31 feet on the true 24 hours and down on the morning; the fetcher's 'rising' flag compares against 97.3 cfs, the first point of the two-day window at 5:45 a.m. Sunday, before the rain, and is wrong on both counts. The read string is the fetcher's flow-band verdict and stands: at 582 cfs on a freestone this size the pocket water is still one sheet, the plunge pools are brown and the undercut banks are under the river, and the line is the bank. The recession has run about 100 cfs a day since Monday morning, so the heavy-nymph-on-the-seam water in the 300-to-500 band is Wednesday or Thursday at that rate and the 100-cfs long-leader water is the weekend at the earliest, if the Flood Watch that now runs through Wednesday morning over Webster and Nicholas counties does not put the gauge back up; a second rise would start from 582, not from base. When it does drop into the seams, the forage after a flush is big and low: stonefly nymphs and sculpin washed off the bottom, fished on the inside edges of the plunge-pool tailouts and along the undercut banks where the current first slackens, before anything on top.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 28.79 feet at 5 a.m., rising, up from 25.55 in the fetcher's comparison. 28.8 ft and rising — up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up and pushing, and the direction is the trace's as well as the fetcher's. The two-day record bottomed at 25.10 feet at 10:15 p.m. Sunday, turned up at 2 a.m. Monday and climbed 2.3 feet by noon, 27.39; it held between 27.37 and 27.42 through the afternoon, then went again from 5 p.m., 27.51 at 6, 28.09 at 10, 28.33 at midnight, 28.56 at 3 a.m. and 28.79 at 5, about a tenth an hour and still going at the fetch. Against the true 24-hour mark, 25.97 at 5 a.m. Monday, it is up 2.82 feet; the fetcher's 25.55 is the 5:45 a.m. Sunday reading. The water is coming out of the Kanawha and the Ohio-side creeks under active products: NWS Charleston's Flood Warning on north central Mason County, with Jackson and Wood and four Ohio counties, ran until 9 a.m. today with significant flooding continuing and numerous roads closed at 9:21 p.m., and the Flood Watch now runs through Wednesday morning on Mason, Putnam, Kanawha, Cabell and the rest of the valley. The pool is carrying color and debris from the Kanawha side and it will not clear while the creeks run. On a pool up nearly three feet and climbing, the fish are off the open faces and in the slack, as the fetcher says: the downstream eddies of the wing dams, the inside of the riprap points and the mouths of the barge cuts, where shad and skipjack pushed off the main flow stack up. A white swimbait or a chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait worked slowly through the eddy behind the wing dam tips is the shad pattern in stained water, and a black-and-blue jig with a crawfish trailer dragged down the riprap is the rock pattern. A bank on a county that was under a Flood Warning at dawn is watched from the truck until the creek mouths stop running brown.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 33.36 feet at 5 a.m., rising, up from 26.36 in the fetcher's comparison. 33.4 ft and rising — up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up almost five feet on the day and still rising, with the crest forecast for this afternoon. The two-day trace held between 28.5 and 28.8 through Sunday night, stood at 28.53 at 5 a.m. Monday, then climbed all day: 29.15 at 9 a.m., 30.20 at 1 p.m., past Sunday's forecast crest of 30.8 at 4 p.m., 31.58 at 7 p.m., 32.02 at 9, 32.68 at midnight, 33.08 at 3 a.m. and 33.36 at 5, the rate easing from about three-tenths an hour in the afternoon to a tenth or so overnight. The Weather Service's NWPS forecast for Huntington, reissued 12:10 a.m. today with the rain in, carries the river to 35.3 feet at 8 a.m., a crest of 35.6 feet at 2 p.m. today holding through 8 p.m., 35.4 at 2 a.m. Wednesday and 34.9 by Wednesday morning, a secondary hold near 34.6 into Thursday, then 33.2 Friday morning, 31.2 Saturday evening and 27.7 by Sunday night, with no flooding category; action stage is 48 feet and minor flood 50, so the crest is more than 12 feet under action. At 33.4 and pushing the river fishes the slack behind anything solid, as the fetcher says: the eddy below the lock wall, the downstream sides of the wing dams, the riprap pockets and the inside of the barge cuts, where shad and skipjack get out of the current. A three-quarter-ounce white swimbait or a skipjack-pattern jerkbait held on the seam where the eddy meets the flow is the pattern for hybrids and white bass on a rise, and a crawfish-colored football jig or a big live shad on a bottom rig in the slack pockets for the catfish that a rising, coloring river turns on. Cabell County is under the Flood Watch through Wednesday morning, and the bank below the dam with a crest still five hours out is not a bank to stand on.USGS 03206000
