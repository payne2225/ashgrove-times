West Virginia

  • Williams River — 350 cfs and falling, 2.55 feet. 350 cfs - pushy. Wadeable at the edges, not across. Off 755 cfs a day ago and still dropping, so the storm water is on its way out rather than blown out. At this height it is a heavy-nymph-on-the-seam problem and not a long-leader one: a weighted stonefly or a sculpin along the undercut banks and the soft edges, and stay out of the main current.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.7 feet and falling. 25.7 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Down four feet on the day and settled at pool, which puts fish back on structure. Shad are the forage: a white swimbait across the wing dams and the riprap is the first look, and the barge cuts hold fish now the colour is clearing.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 28.5 feet and falling. 28.5 ft and falling - up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Off seven and a half feet from yesterday, the biggest drop on the paper this morning, but still high and coloured. Debris is the hazard. The slack behind the lock wall and the rock is where to start, and a crawfish-coloured crankbait tight to the riprap is the second look.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 3:15 AM at 3.0 ft and 3:56 PM at 3.9 ft at Hampstead; lows 9:34 AM at 0.6 ft and 10:34 PM at 1.2 ft. Fish the moving water either side of high at 3:15 AM and 3:56 PM in the sound. Tide before lure. The afternoon high is the bigger of the two at 3.9 feet and the only one in daylight, so the push into it is the window. Mud minnows, shrimp and finger mullet are the forage, so a soft plastic or live bait along the grass edges and the oyster bars while the water moves. Red drum, speckled trout and black drum are open; flounder is not until Sept. 1.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
