  • Williams River — 97.3 cfs, 1.62 feet at 5:15 a.m., steady on flow and stage against 93.8 cfs and 1.6 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 97 cfs - prime wading water. The gauge at Dyer reads 97.3 cfs and steady, about 5 cfs over the 92.1 that Tuesday's paper carried at the same hour and still inside the late-autumn base flow the river has settled into since the weekend bump ran off. At 97 cfs and clear it is a long-leader, small-fly problem, with the fish on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and after another cold night the midday hours fish best once the sun is on the water. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a long leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water, and a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks in the first and last hour of light.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.79 feet at 5 a.m., falling against 25.05 in the fetcher's comparison. 24.8 ft and falling — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with ordinary bank access, down about a quarter of a foot in 24 hours and 18 hundredths under Tuesday's printed reading. The wing dams and the riprap are showing rock and the water is clear enough to fish structure rather than the slack behind it: shad are the forage, so a white or shad-pattern swimbait or a lipless crankbait worked down the face of the wing dams and along the riprap is the pattern, slowed down for water that saw frost inland again overnight, with a jig and crawfish on the slack side of the lock wall for the fish holding off the current.
