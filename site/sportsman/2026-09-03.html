On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 65.6 cfs and falling, 1.42 feet. 66 cfs - prime wading water. Down from 101 a day ago and thinner every morning this week - the river is a string of pools now, with the riffles between them ankle-deep. Fish it from below on your knees: a 12-foot leader, one beetle or ant dropped under the overhanging bank, and a small olive sculpin swung through the two or three plunge pools still holding depth. Sculpin and caddis are the forage here, and at this flow the caddis are what the fish see.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.8 feet and steady. 24.8 ft - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Pool water, unchanged in a day - the wing-dam tips and the riprap are the structure, shad the forage, so a white swimbait or a spoon over the tips early and a football jig dragged down the seam once the sun is on the water.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 25.81 feet and rising. 25.8 ft and rising - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Up three-tenths in a day below the dam, so there is a little more current on the lock wall and the barge cuts - crawfish colors on a jig pitched into the cuts, and a skipjack-pattern swimbait worked along the riprap where the flow pushes bait against it.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 1:17 AM at 3.4 ft and 1:57 PM at 4.3 ft at Hampstead; lows 7:36 AM at 0.4 ft and 8:45 PM at 0.9 ft. Fish the moving water either side of high at 1:17 AM and 1:57 PM in the sound. The Fisherman's Post September report, published Tuesday and two days old, has reds and specks on topwater at dock lights, creek mouths and grass banks early, flounder "going wild" at 20 to 24 inches on bucktails with soft plastics, and Spanish and blues hit-or-miss on Gotchas off the piers. Flounder want moving water on structure, so work the flood into the 1:57 PM high; reds want the last of the fall, into tonight's 8:45 PM low. Waning gibbous at 61%, easing toward the neap. Water 85.6F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
