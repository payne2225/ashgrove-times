Around the Leagues
Hoshoryu forces down Asanoyama for a second straight win at the Kyushu basho

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu, back after right knee surgery kept him out of September's tournament and the end of July's, beat maegashira (rank-and-file) 1 Asanoyama by yoritaoshi (force out and down) in 5.1 seconds in the final bout of day 2 at the Fukuoka Kokusai Center, taking a left front-mawashi grip, letting go of his overarm hold and swinging Asanoyama off balance with a right underarm throw before driving him down; he said he had been nervous on day 1 after the long layoff and could concentrate on day 2. Hoshoryu has won all six meetings with Asanoyama, who had beaten ozeki (champion) Aonishiki on day 1.

Nikkan Sports
Onosato survives his own pull as both yokozuna and all three ozeki win on day 2

Yokozuna Onosato, beaten on day 1, got his right hand inside against maegashira 1 Fujiryoga, then pressed down on his head and pulled, drawing Fujiryoga into his chest and onto the edge before he circled, got the right arm back in and won by yorikiri (force out) in 9.6 seconds; Kasugano, the former sekiwake Tochinowaka commentating for NHK, said pulling while pressing the head is something a yokozuna must not do. Ozeki Aonishiki beat Yoshinofuji, day 1's conqueror of Onosato, by uchimuso (inner thigh twist down) in 5.0 seconds, Kotozakura slapped down Churanoumi in 4.2 seconds and Kirishima forced out Fujiseiun for his second win; chief judge Kokonoe, the former ozeki Chiyotaikai, said it was not a day with an upset in the air.

Fujinokawa falls to a second straight loss; Tamawashi, 41, passes Kisenosato with his 715th win

Sekiwake Fujinokawa, 21, who wanted this tournament to build a case for ozeki promotion after 11 wins in September, was caught at the tachiai (initial charge) by maegashira 3 Takayasu, who locked his arms and walked him out by kimedashi (arm-lock force out) in 8.7 seconds. Tamawashi, 41, the oldest man to return to the top division since the Showa era, thrust out Tokihayate for his first win and his 715th in makuuchi (the top division), moving past the former yokozuna Kisenosato into 7th on the all-time list a week before his 42nd birthday; Nikkan Sports' results table has eight men unbeaten after two days, among them Hoshoryu, Kirishima, Atamifuji and Takanosho. In juryo (the second division), Wakatakakage pushed out Namatame for a second win since returning from four leg operations, and Hishoshi withdrew with a fractured left eye socket, the second kyujo (absence) in the top two divisions.
