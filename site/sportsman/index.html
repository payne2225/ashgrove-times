West Virginia

  • Williams River — 139 cfs, 1.83 feet at 5:15 a.m., rising on both flow and stage against 42.0 cfs and 1.23 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 139 cfs - prime wading water. The rise that began Thursday has carried the gauge at Dyer to more than three times its 24-hour-ago reading, and the stage is up six tenths of a foot on the day; the fetcher still reads it as prime wading water, and 139 cfs is a comfortable wade on the Williams, but it is rising water with the first real colour of the fall in it, so the trout are off the shallow pocket-water lies and on the seams and the soft inside edges of the plunge pools. That makes it a heavy-nymph-on-the-seam morning rather than a long-leader problem: a stonefly or a caddis pupa under enough weight to find the bottom of the pools, and with the water up and off-colour a sculpin or a small black streamer swung tight to the undercut banks. If the rise continues through the day the pools will fish better than the pocket water, and the wading gets harder than the number suggests.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.82 feet at 5 a.m., falling against 25.08 in the fetcher's comparison. 24.8 ft and falling — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with ordinary bank access, a quarter of a foot lower than this time Friday, and the lowest the pool side has read this week: the wing dams and the riprap are showing rock. Shad are the forage, so a white or shad-pattern swimbait or a lipless crankbait worked down the exposed face of the wing dams and along the riprap is the pattern on a falling pool, with a jig and crawfish on the slack side of the lock wall for the fish that have pulled off the structure as the water dropped. The R.C. Byrd tailwater itself is not gauged; this is the pool side.USGS 03201500 (Ohio River at Point Pleasant); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 25.93 feet at 5 a.m., steady against 25.85 in the fetcher's comparison. 25.9 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool and the steadiest of the three gauges, within a tenth of a foot of Friday's reading while the pool above the dam falls. Below the dam the pattern is the structure the dam makes: skipjack and shad stack in the current below the gates, and a spoon or a heavy jig worked in the churn, or a live skipjack drifted along the current seams off the lock wall, is the hybrid and catfish setup at this stage; the riprap along the Huntington bank fishes a crawfish-colour jig on the slack side where the bank current breaks. Huntington is the downstream bracket, 30-odd river miles below the dam, not the tailwater reading, which the Corps publish and this pipeline cannot fetch.USGS 03206000 (Ohio River at Huntington); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
