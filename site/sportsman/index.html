Chelsea beat Leeds 6-3 after trailing 2-0 at the break in the Carabao Cup

Leeds led through Tarik Muharemovic's 23rd-minute header and Brenden Aaronson in the 48th before Xabi Alonso's four halftime changes turned it at Stamford Bridge: Cole Palmer scored a 53rd-minute penalty and again in the 55th, Pedro Neto in the 60th, Danny Welbeck in the 65th and in stoppage time, and Valentin Barco his first Chelsea goal in the 80th; Dominic Calvert-Lewin pulled one back for Leeds. Morgan Rogers assisted four. 'I take responsibility with the first half,' Alonso said. Chelsea advance to the fourth round.

Elly De La Cruz's 26th homer, on Yamamoto's first pitch of the fourth, was the Reds' only run and made it a career-high five straight games with one; Mookie Betts, Max Muncy, Kyle Tucker and Enrique Hernandez homered for the Dodgers, who have won seven straight. The Reds are 69-77, fifth in the NL Central, and open at Milwaukee Friday at 7:45 p.m. ET.

Around the Leagues
Hoshoryu skips practice a second day; his Aki entry is decided Friday morning

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu missed morning practice Wednesday and Thursday for medical appointments and has taken no bouts since his July 29 right-knee surgery; stablemaster Tatsunami said all he has managed is stomping shiko and giving his chest in butsukari-geiko (pushing drills). Kyodo says the decision comes at Friday morning's bout-drawing meeting in Tokyo, Thursday evening in the East; sitting out would be a third straight basho (tournament) missed and his first day-one withdrawal. Chunichi Sports calls his chances of entering 'extremely difficult.' The basho opens Sunday at Ryogoku Kokugikan, three days out.

Kyodo News
Kirishima closes his tune-up by winning 19 of 24 at Arashio stable

Ozeki (champion) Kirishima, on a yokozuna promotion run, called his own final visiting practice Thursday in Tokyo and took 24 straight bouts against new sekiwake (junior champion) Fujinokawa, komusubi Daieisho and maegashira Abi, winning 19; after one loss to Daieisho he shouted for one more and won it. He said his movement and the sharpness of his charge 'weren't as good as yesterday,' when he won 19 of 21, and that all he can do now 'is rest well and go into the tournament.'

On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 47.6 cfs, 1.28 feet, falling. 48 cfs - prime wading water. Down from 69.9 cfs at this hour yesterday and about as low and clear as the river gets in September. At 48 cfs this is a long-leader, small-fly river: a 12-foot leader to 6X, a size 16 or 18 caddis or a small stonefly nymph through the pocket water and the plunge pools, caddis, stoneflies and sculpin being the forage, and a small olive sculpin along the undercut banks before the sun is on the water. NWS Charleston's 2:31 a.m. discussion has rounds of showers and storms ahead of a cold front through early Friday with flash flooding the main concern in training storms; the gridded forecast for Cowen sums to about half an inch today and under a third of an inch Friday, so the river should stay fishable unless a storm trains over the headwaters.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.15 feet, steady. 25.1 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. The gauge read normal pool at 5 a.m., but the water is about to change: NWS Charleston issued a flash flood warning for northern Mason County including Point Pleasant, Leon, Henderson and Lakin at 3:10 a.m. and extended it at 5:39 a.m. to 8:15 a.m., saying 3 to 4 inches had fallen with up to an inch more possible. The Kanawha and the small tributaries on both banks will put color into the pool side through the weekend. Before it arrives, this is still structure water: shad are the forage, so a white or pearl swimbait over the wing-dam tips at first light, then a football jig dragged into the slack behind them; once the creeks come in, the bite moves to the mudlines where a colored creek meets the clearer pool.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 25.79 feet, falling. 25.8 ft and falling — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. The Weather Service reissued the Huntington forecast at 10:25 a.m. Wednesday, and it now has a modest rise instead of the slow fall it carried Tuesday: 26.0 feet by 8 a.m. today, 26.3 by Friday morning and a 26.8-foot crest early Saturday, falling back to 26.1 by Monday, no flooding, with the action stage at 48 feet. That is a foot of rise over two days, enough to put current on the lock wall and color along the banks. Shad and skipjack are the forage, so a skipjack-pattern swimbait or a white spinnerbait along the riprap and the lock wall early today while it is still clear, and a crawfish-colored jig in the slack behind the barge cuts and the wing-dam tips as the river comes up.USGS 03206000
