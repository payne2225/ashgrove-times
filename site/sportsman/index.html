Brady reported to buy into Clearlake, the firm that controls Chelsea

Tom Brady, the seven-time Super Bowl winner, has bought a stake in Chelsea, ESPN reported Friday, citing sources who confirmed the purchase to ESPN and NFL Network; the report says the deal makes him a minority investor in the Clearlake Capital vehicle that controls the club and that he is expected to serve as an operating adviser to Clearlake without voting rights at Chelsea. Neither Chelsea nor Clearlake had replied to ESPN's request for comment, so the purchase stands as reported, not confirmed by the club. Brady left the ownership group of second-tier Birmingham City earlier this month after holding a 3.3% stake; Clearlake took full control of Chelsea in September when Todd Boehly and Mark Walter sold their stakes, in a deal the Financial Times put at 950 million pounds for their combined holding and a club valuation of 5 billion pounds including debt. Chelsea host Leeds at Stamford Bridge today at 10 a.m. ET, their first match since the international break.

North Carolina beats Marshall 88-72 as Able scores 23 and Fens makes his debut

North Carolina defeated Marshall 88-72 at the Dean E. Smith Center in Chapel Hill on Friday night before 16,391, per ESPN's box score and the AP game story. The Tar Heels led 25-8 with 11 and a half minutes left in the first half before the Herd closed to 42-37 at the break, then pulled away again in the second half and led by as many as 17. Matt Able scored 23 with five threes, Terrence Brown 20 with six rebounds and five assists, Jarin Stevenson 13, and 7-foot South Dakota transfer Cameron Fens had nine points and a team-high eight rebounds in his North Carolina debut, two days after a judge granted him a temporary restraining order in his eligibility suit that lets him play at least through a Nov. 30 hearing. Marshall is 2-4 and next hosts Prairie View A&M on Wednesday at 4 p.m. ET; North Carolina is 4-1.

Nashville's Callaghan says his focus is on the playoffs as U.S. Soccer links grow

BJ Callaghan, the Nashville SC coach who ran the U.S. men's national team on an interim basis in 2023, said Friday that his focus is 100% on the players, the staff and the club through the MLS Cup Playoffs, ESPN reported, after he was linked to the vacancy left when Mauricio Pochettino departed for Manchester United this week. Callaghan won the Concacaf Nations League as interim coach in 2023 and led Nashville to a record 77-point Supporters' Shield season; his side opens a best-of-three Round One series against FC Cincinnati on Sunday at 4 p.m. ET. ESPN names Pep Guardiola as the biggest name linked to the job and says under-23 coach Steve Cherundolo will run the December camp and a Dec. 13 friendly against Jamaica while the search continues. Pochettino, at his first Manchester United news conference Friday, said the pressure of coaching the U.S. at the World Cup was massive and that he arrives a better coach for it, per the AP.

Onosato and Wakamotoharu go to the final day level at 12 wins after both win on day 14

Yokozuna (grand champion) Onosato forced out maegashira (rank-and-file) 7 Kinbozan by yorikiri (frontal force-out) and maegashira 7 Wakamotoharu forced down ozeki (champion) Aonishiki by yoritaoshi (frontal crush-out) on day 14 of the Kyushu basho (tournament) in Fukuoka on Saturday, leaving the two level at 12 wins and two losses with one day left, per the Japan Sumo Association's tournament leaders page and the SumoDB results table. Sekiwake (third-highest rank) Atamifuji threw down ozeki Kirishima by sukuinage (beltless arm throw) and is alone a win behind at 11 and three; Kinbozan, maegashira 1 Asanoyama and maegashira 11 Asahakuryu are at 10 and four. Aonishiki fell to six wins and eight losses, a make-koshi (losing record) for the ozeki, and yokozuna Hoshoryu tripped ozeki Kotozakura by sotogake (outside leg trip) to reach nine wins. The senshuraku (final day) pairings Sunday do not put the leaders together: Onosato meets Hoshoryu and Wakamotoharu meets Atamifuji, so if the two finish level the yusho (championship) goes to a playoff bout, and Atamifuji can still force his way in by beating Wakamotoharu while Onosato loses. In juryo (the second division) Wakatakakage beat Ryuden to reach 13 wins and one loss with Asahifuji a win behind at 12 and two.

  • Williams River — 93.8 cfs, 1.6 feet at 5:15 a.m., falling on flow against 99.1 cfs and steady on stage against 1.63 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 94 cfs - prime wading water. At 94 cfs and clear it is a long-leader, small-fly problem, the fish on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and after another cold night the midday hours fish best once the sun is on the water. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a long leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water, and a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks in the first and last hour of light. The Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry report, a monthly, is dated Nov. 1 and is 20 days old, so it is the month's pattern and not this morning's conditions: East Coast Sports reports the piers taking big red drum on the north end of the island, smaller reds and the occasional trout in the sound and surf trout showing up; Native Son says the deeper seams in the main creeks are the trout target and cut bait the best bet for the reds inside; Speckled Specialist reports live shrimp floated under a cork as the best approach, 3/16-ounce jig heads with a soft plastic for artificials and black drum and small reds on the same floated shrimp; Carolina Flats has topwater for locating trout early and soft plastics for reds around the oysters and grass banks; Jolly Roger Pier reports speckled trout starting to show in small numbers, mullet and spot at night and thick bluefish early; Surf City Pier has pompano, black drum and croaker on the bottom. Tied to today's table and a sound in the low 60s, the 11:31 low falls at midday and the window is the top of the flood and the first of the ebb, roughly 2 to 6 p.m., once the sun has been on the flats: trout in the 5-to-8-foot creek-channel seams and along the ICWW channel edge on a 3/16-ounce jig head and a small soft plastic worked slowly, or a live shrimp under a popping cork drifted over the oyster bars at the creek mouths as the water starts to leave the grass after 5:16. Red drum want the last of the fall, roughly 9 p.m. to the 11:36 low, well into dark on a 0.1-foot bottom that will drain the creek mouths hard: cut menhaden or a finger mullet on a quarter-ounce head a couple of feet off the creek-channel edges, or a soft plastic on the oyster and grass banks in the last of the light; the flooded-grass window for a weedless gold spoon is the hour before the 5:16 high, which now falls at dusk. Spot and sea mullet on bloodworm or shrimp on a two-hook bottom rig in the ICWW channel on the last of the flood; with 4-to-6-foot breakers under the advisory from 1 p.m., the beach and pier bite is the early morning only, bluefish on a metal jig before the swell builds.
