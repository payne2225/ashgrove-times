Alonso keeps faith in Penders and names him to start at Anfield

Xabi Alonso confirmed on Monday that Mike Penders will start Wednesday's Carabao Cup fourth-round match at Liverpool, 4 p.m. ET at Anfield, two days after the 21-year-old goalkeeper punched a corner into his own net in Chelsea's 2-0 home loss to Tottenham, his first Premier League start. "It's part of the business; you need to deal with mistakes and we keep confidence in Mike," Alonso told the club's website. "We win and we lose together." Maxence Lacroix said the squad is "behind him" and called Wednesday "a big game for us. We want to go far in this competition." Cole Palmer serves his one-match ban for Saturday's red card at Anfield and is due back for Manchester United's visit to Stamford Bridge on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET. Chelsea remain 5th in the Premier League, two points behind Liverpool.

Van Dijk says Liverpool's new forwards must now deliver every three days

Virgil van Dijk said Monday that nobody can replicate what Mohamed Salah did for Liverpool but that the forwards who replaced him "have to step up," after the 2-0 win over Brighton on Sunday, the first match in which Alexander Isak, Bradley Barcola, Florian Wirtz and Cody Gakpo looked settled together. "At a club like Liverpool you have to do it every three or four days," the captain said. "I'm pretty confident that it is going to be fine." Isak has scored in six straight league matches; Salah has 10 goals in 10 for Trabzonspor. Van Dijk also praised 21-year-old center back Jeremy Jacquet, signed from Rennes. Liverpool, 4th in the Premier League with four clean sheets, host Chelsea in the Carabao Cup fourth round on Wednesday at 4 p.m. ET and Arsenal in the league on Sunday at 11:30 a.m. ET. There was no update Monday on Wirtz, who went off in the 65th minute Sunday.

Kane thanks Tottenham as he becomes the first English Ballon d'Or winner since 2001

Harry Kane, who joined Tottenham at 11 and left for Bayern Munich in 2023 as the club's record scorer, used his acceptance speech at Monday's Ballon d'Or ceremony in London to thank his former club. "I wouldn't be where I am today without Tottenham, for sure," Kane said. "All the guidance, the coaches, you allowed me to live my dream." He singled out Chris Ramsey, Tim Sherwood and Ryan Mason among the coaches who worked with him at Spurs. Kane, 33, beat Kylian Mbappe and Lamine Yamal to the award and is the first Englishman to win it since Michael Owen in 2001 and the fifth overall; he captained England to third place at the 2026 World Cup. Wembley lit its arch gold afterward. Tottenham, 14th in the Premier League, host Crystal Palace on Saturday at 1:30 p.m. ET.

Browns activate Gabriel from injured reserve and carry 4 quarterbacks

Cleveland activated Dillon Gabriel from injured reserve Monday, a day after Sunday's 27-24 win at Tennessee, putting four quarterbacks on the 53-man roster: Deshaun Watson, who has started all seven games, backup Shedeur Sanders, rookie Taylen Green and Gabriel, a 2025 third-round pick who went on IR with a back injury at the August cutdown. Brewers rout Rays 10-1 with an eight-run fifth, one win from a first title

Milwaukee scored a World Series-record eight two-out runs in the fifth inning Monday night at Tropicana Field, seven straight hits, and leads the Series three games to none. Jackson Chourio went 4 for 5 with a double and a stolen base, Brice Turang's two-run single off Cam Booser opened the barrage after Freddy Peralta left with the bases loaded, and Cooper Pratt and Sal Frelick doubled in three more; the Brewers had 16 hits, none of them home runs. Rookie left-hander Robert Gasser allowed one hit and faced the minimum through six innings, the first World Series starter to do so since Curt Schilling in 2001, and Brandon Woodruff pitched the ninth in his first appearance since July 4. Only the 2004 Red Sox have come back from three games down in a best-of-seven. Game 4 is Tuesday at 8 p.m. ET on Fox, Griffin Jax starting for Tampa Bay; Milwaukee had not named a starter.

Cowboys beat Eagles 38-21 on Williams' 155 yards; Philadelphia drops fifth straight

Oregon rules out Moore for the season; Baylor and NC State turn to interim coaches

Hoshoryu says he intends to compete in Kyushu after 15 bouts on his repaired knee

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu, 27, took 15 bouts against makushita (third-division) and lower wrestlers at the Tatsunami stable's lodging in Itoshima, Fukuoka, on Tuesday, winning 13, in his first practice since the banzuke (rankings) came out, Nikkan Sports reported. He had right knee surgery in late July and sat out all of September; the knee was taped and in a brace. "I am working with the intention of competing," he said of the Kyushu basho (tournament), which opens Nov. 8 at the Fukuoka Kokusai Center, 12 days out, adding that he would not know until the last minute, would decide with his stablemaster and wanted above all not to make it worse. Sports Hochi quoted him saying the knee is improving day by day; he joined the autumn jungyo (regional tour) partway through and stayed to its Oct. 25 close. The Japan Sumo Association's kyujo (absence) page lists no withdrawals.
