Around the Leagues
Atamifuji topples Onosato and shares the Kyushu lead with Wakamotoharu after Day 10

The Japan Sumo Association's tournament leaders page lists two rikishi (wrestlers) at nine wins and one loss after day 10 of the Kyushu basho (tournament) in Fukuoka on Tuesday, sekiwake Atamifuji and maegashira (rank-and-file) 7 Wakamotoharu, with four at eight wins and two losses: yokozuna (grand champion) Onosato, ozeki (champion) Kirishima, maegashira 7 Kinbozan and maegashira 14 Dewanoryu. Per the SumoDB results table for the day, Atamifuji pushed out Onosato for his second win over a yokozuna in two days after beating Hoshoryu on Monday, handing Onosato a second loss; ozeki Aonishiki threw Kirishima with an underarm throw to knock him off the lead and reach six wins; Wakamotoharu forced out Asahakuryu for an eighth straight win; yokozuna Hoshoryu sidestepped Takerufuji at the charge and won by tsukiotoshi (thrust down) to even his record at five wins and five losses, sending Takerufuji to a make-koshi (losing record) with an eighth loss; ozeki Kotozakura was forced out by maegashira 3 Hakunofuji and is also at five and five; and komusubi Churanoumi, the former Mimoumi, fell to a ninth loss. Kotoeiho returned from six days out with a herniated cervical disc and beat Daieisho. In juryo (the second division) all three leaders lost: Onokatsu beat Wakatakakage to end his nine-bout run, Enho beat Tamashoho and Kagayaki beat Asahifuji, leaving the three level at nine wins and one loss on the JSA table. The final day is Sunday, Nov. 22.

Chargers beat the Ravens 23-20 in Baltimore for their first win of the season

Justin Herbert ran 22 yards for a first-quarter touchdown and Omarion Hampton carried 22 times for 105 yards and a score as Los Angeles beat Baltimore 23-20 at M&T Bank Stadium on Monday night before 69,679, per ESPN's box score. The line score was Los Angeles 10, 7, 3, 3 against Baltimore 0, 10, 3, 7: Cameron Dicker kicked field goals of 38, 34 and 35 yards, and Derrick Henry scored on runs of 2 and 1 yards, the second with 10:23 left, for a Ravens offense held to 10 rushing yards on his 10 carries. Herbert was 12 of 18 for 129 yards and Tre' Harris caught five for 86; Dalvin Tomlinson had two sacks. The Ravens had more yards, 347 to 287, and more first downs, 22 to 15, but gave the ball away twice while the Chargers had no turnovers. The Chargers are 1-8; the Ravens are 4-6 and have dropped five straight.

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 92.1 cfs, 1.59 feet at 5:15 a.m., falling on flow and steady on stage against 106 cfs and 1.67 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 92 cfs - prime wading water. The fetcher reads the gauge at Dyer as falling, but Monday's paper carried 93.8 cfs and 1.6 feet at the same hour, so the river has given up less than 2 cfs in 24 hours and the 106 cfs the comparison names is Sunday's reading, the 48-hour quirk again: the Saturday bump is spent and the Williams is settling toward its late-autumn base flow. At 92 cfs it is a long-leader, small-fly problem in low, clear water, with the fish on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and after another frosty night the midday hours fish best once the sun is on the water. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a long leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water, and a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks in the first and last hour of light.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.97 feet at 5 a.m., steady against 25.03 in the fetcher's comparison. 25.0 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with ordinary bank access, six hundredths of a foot under Monday's printed reading and steady. The wing dams and the riprap are showing rock and the water is clear enough to fish structure rather than the slack behind it: shad are the forage, so a white or shad-pattern swimbait or a lipless crankbait worked down the face of the wing dams and along the riprap is the pattern, slowed down for water that saw frost inland again overnight, with a jig and crawfish on the slack side of the lock wall for the fish holding off the current. The R.C. Byrd tailwater itself is not gauged; this is the pool side.USGS 03201500 (Ohio River at Point Pleasant); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.0 feet at 5 a.m., steady against 26.17 in the fetcher's comparison. 26.0 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool and steady, down from the 26.56 feet and rising that Monday's paper carried: the water the dam was passing on Monday has moved through and the lower gauge is back where it sat on Sunday. Below the dam the pattern is the structure the dam makes: skipjack and shad stack in the current below the gates, and a spoon or a heavy jig worked in the churn, or a live skipjack drifted along the current seams off the lock wall, is the hybrid and catfish setup, with less current to work with than Monday; the riprap along the Huntington bank fishes a crawfish-colour jig on the slack side where the bank current breaks. Huntington is the downstream bracket, 30-odd river miles below the dam, not the tailwater reading, which the Corps publish and this pipeline cannot fetch.USGS 03206000 (Ohio River at Huntington); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
