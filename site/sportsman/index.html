  • Williams River — 397 cfs, 2.67 feet at 5:15 a.m., both trends reading rising in the fetcher's comparison against 71.4 cfs and 1.46 feet. 397 cfs - pushy. Wadeable at the edges, not across. The gauge at Dyer reads 397 cfs and 2.67 feet at 5:15 a.m. and the fetcher's comparison calls it rising against 71.4 cfs, but the 71.4 is the pre-storm number, the Thursday 6 a.m. reading, and the USGS instantaneous record tells the other story: the river sat near 71 to 100 cfs all Thanksgiving Day, jumped from 164 cfs at 10 p.m. Thursday to 644 by 10:45 p.m., crested at 726 cfs between 12:30 and 12:45 a.m. Friday, and has fallen every hour since, 612 at noon Friday, 451 at midnight, 397 this morning, down 329 cfs from the crest over 29 hours and dropping about 20 cfs an hour overnight. That is a river receding from a one-night surge, not one coming up, and the stage has fallen about half a foot since Friday's paper. The fetcher's verdict is the paper's: 397 cfs - pushy. Wadeable at the edges, not across. At this flow, still carrying colour, the pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks the config records are a heavy-nymph-on-the-seam problem, not a long-leader small-fly one: a big dark stonefly nymph on point with a caddis larva on the dropper, stoneflies and caddis being the forage, weighted to the bottom and fished short on a tight line in the soft inside seams and the tails of the plunge pools that can be reached from the near bank; a sculpin streamer on a sink tip swung through the slack under the undercut banks in the first light, sculpin being the third forage the config lists and the one a dropping, coloured river puts on the move.
