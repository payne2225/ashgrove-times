Palmer is suspended for Wednesday's cup trip to Liverpool

Cole Palmer's first red card as a senior player, a second yellow for a late tackle on Conor Gallagher in the 80th minute of Saturday's home loss to Tottenham, carries an automatic one-match ban that falls on Chelsea's Carabao Cup fourth-round match at Anfield on Wednesday at 4 p.m. ET, so Palmer will be available for Manchester United's visit to Stamford Bridge on Saturday unless the suspension is extended. The dismissal was Chelsea's 100th in the Premier League, not counting rescinded cards; only Everton (113) and Arsenal (110) have more. Chelsea's players and staff were sent off 11 times last season, the most in the league, with Tottenham next on five. Palmer had been booked in the 70th for pushing his midriff into Mateus Fernandes' face as the midfielder lay on the ground. Chelsea dropped to 5th on Sunday when Liverpool, Wednesday's opponent, beat Brighton.

Liverpool beat Brighton 2-0 for a first home league win under Iraola

Liverpool scored twice in the first 21 minutes at Anfield on Sunday and won 2-0, their fourth straight victory in all competitions and their first home Premier League win since beating Crystal Palace in April. Florian Wirtz's 17th-minute shot went in off Pascal Gross for an own goal from Ronald Araujo's cross, and Alexander Isak turned in Bradley Barcola's cross from the right of the six-yard box in the 21st, his sixth goal in seven league appearances. Brighton had 64.4 percent of possession and 17 shots to 13 but put one on target; Yasin Ayari hit the post in the first half. Wirtz went off injured in the 65th minute after treatment at the hour mark. Fabian Hurzeler said "there was one team who really tried to create"; Andoni Iraola said "the key was how we started." Liverpool rise to 4th on 15 points and host Chelsea in the Carabao Cup on Wednesday at 4 p.m. ET.

Burrow throws 4 TD passes as Bengals erase 13-0 deficit to beat Ravens 35-13

Cincinnati was outgained 258 yards to 59 in the first half in Baltimore on Sunday and trailed 13-0, then scored touchdowns on its first five possessions after halftime and won 35-13 before 70,464 at M&T Bank Stadium. Joe Burrow went 22 of 30 for 292 yards and touchdown passes of 6 yards to Mike Gesicki, 13 to Tee Higgins, 2 to Ja'Marr Chase and 16 to Drew Sample; Samaje Perine ran in from the 1. Higgins caught six passes for 148 yards, all after halftime. Chase said he screamed at the offense at halftime that "somebody's got to make a play for us." Lamar Jackson missed a third straight game with his ankle injury; Tyler Huntley threw for 298 yards. Baltimore became the third team in NFL history to lead by 13 or more at the half and lose by 20 or more, per ESPN Research. The Bengals, 4-2 and third in the AFC North, host Tennessee on Sunday at 1 p.m. ET.

Browns beat Titans 27-24 on Szmyt's field goal as time expires

Deshaun Watson hit Harold Fannin Jr. for 36 yards to set up Andre Szmyt's 32-yard field goal on the final play, and Cleveland won 27-24 in Nashville, Tennessee, on Sunday for a fifth win in six games. Watson, who led his fifth fourth-quarter winning drive of the season, completed 25 of 36 passes for 308 yards and ran 9 yards for the touchdown that put Cleveland up 24-17 with 4:34 left; Tony Pollard's third touchdown run, with 58 seconds remaining, tied it. Quinshon Judkins, back from an ankle injury, ran for 96 yards and two scores, and rookie KC Concepcion caught eight passes for 129 yards; Titans coach Robert Saleh said officials appeared to miss a false start on the Fannin play. Separately, Maliek Collins' agent told ESPN on Sunday that the defensive tackle agreed to a two-year, $30 million extension with $23 million guaranteed. The Browns, 5-2 and tied atop the AFC North with Pittsburgh, visit the Steelers on Sunday at 1 p.m. ET.

Kyushu banzuke keeps Onosato and Hoshoryu as yokozuna, lifts Aonishiki to top ozeki

The Japan Sumo Association released the banzuke (rankings) Monday for the Kyushu basho (tournament), which opens Nov. 8 at the Fukuoka Kokusai Center, 13 days out. Onosato, 26, the September champion with 12 wins in 15 bouts, is East yokozuna (grand champion) and Hoshoryu, 27, who had right knee surgery after July and missed all of September, remains West yokozuna; Nikkan Sports reported he joined the autumn tour partway through and that whether he competes is still being watched, with no kyujo (absence) announced. Aonishiki, 22, runner-up with 11 wins, moves to East ozeki (champion) No. 1 ahead of Kotozakura and Kirishima. Fujinokawa and Atamifuji are the sekiwake (junior champions); Minoumi, 33, becomes the first Okinawa-born rikishi (wrestler) to reach komusubi (the lowest titled rank), and Tamawashi, 41, returns to the top division as the oldest wrestler to regain a top-division rank since the start of the Showa era in 1926.
