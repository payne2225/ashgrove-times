West Virginia

  • Williams River — 179 cfs and falling, 2.0 feet. 179 cfs - prime wading water. First reading under 200 cfs since the Aug. 16 flood, off 258 a day ago and clearing. Back to pocket-water fishing: a caddis or stonefly nymph on a long leader through the plunge pools, dries where fish show against the undercut banks.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.06 feet, falling. 25.1 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Pool and settling, which stacks fish on structure. Shad are the forage: a white swimbait worked across the wing dams and riprap seams.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 27.79 feet and falling. 27.8 ft and falling - up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Down two and a half feet on the day but still coloured. Stay on the slack behind rock and the lock wall; a crawfish-coloured crankbait tight to the riprap until it greens up.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 5:48 AM at 3.1 ft and 6:30 PM at 4.0 ft at Hampstead; lows 12:07 AM at 1.1 ft and 12:01 PM at 0.5 ft. Fish the moving water either side of high at 5:48 AM and 6:30 PM in the sound. Tide before lure. The fall off the dawn high runs to a half-foot midday low - the long ebb is the red drum window, the last of it over the oyster bars and grass edges with cut menhaden or finger mullet. An 85% waxing gibbous is building toward full, so spring tides will stiffen the inlet current through the week. The Fisherman's Post monthly, published Aug. 3 and three weeks old, has whiting and black drum in the surf on shrimp and sand fleas, tarpon to 110 pounds off the Jolly Roger's king rigs, and specks, pompano and Spanish at Surf City pier. Flounder stays catch-and-release until Sept. 1.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
