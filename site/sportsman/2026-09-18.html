  • Williams River — 56.2 cfs, 1.35 feet, falling, five days off the Sunday-evening crest and back at its pre-rain base. 56 cfs - prime wading water. Down 2.6 cfs and two-hundredths of a foot from the 58.8 and 1.37 this paper printed at this hour Thursday, and exactly the 56.2 and 1.35 it printed for Sunday morning before the rain, so the river has given back the whole bump. The fetcher's falling flag compares against Wednesday morning's 72.9 and 1.47, the first point of the two-day trace, and it agrees with the 24-hour direction; the trace also shows the river has been within 3 cfs of this number for 20 hours. At base, this is the long-leader, small-fly problem in full: caddis, stoneflies and sculpin are the forage, and pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks the structure, which at 1.35 feet means a 12-foot leader tapered to 6X, a size 16 to 18 caddis dry or elk-hair emerger drifted through the pocket heads and the tailouts of the plunge pools, a size 16 or 18 stonefly nymph or pheasant tail a foot under it in the seams still carrying current, and a small olive sculpin swung under the undercut banks in the first low light, because at this stage every fish in the pockets can see a shadow on the water. The day is the wet one of the week: NWS Charleston's forecast for Cowen, updated 5:19 a.m., has showers and thunderstorms at an 80% chance, mostly cloudy with a high near 75, a northwest wind of 1 to 5 and a tenth to a quarter of an inch of rain, then a 30% chance before 8 p.m. with a low around 61, and Saturday mostly sunny near 77 with only a slight chance of storms after 5 p.m. A quarter inch on a river at base shows as color at the creek mouths before it shows as flow, and the cloud cover is the one thing in the forecast that helps a wading angler at 1.35 feet.USGS 03186500

Neap water: the moon is six and a half days past new, at first quarter by the fetcher's phase, and the quarter is when the sun and moon pull at right angles, so the inlet current is as gentle as it gets in the cycle and the smallest swings run a day or two behind the quarter, into the weekend. The sound swings 3.1 feet from the 8:02 a.m. low to the 2:31 p.m. high, the same range as Thursday, and the ocean station 3.4 feet against 3.7 Thursday and 4.2 Wednesday, so the inlet is softening a notch a day; the afternoon high in the sound holds at 4.0 feet and the evening low at 9:03 p.m. sits a tenth higher than Thursday's at 1.4. The sound today runs 57 minutes behind the inlet on the midday high, 67 on the overnight high, and 83 and 50 minutes behind on the morning and evening lows, so the flood in the backwater runs from 8:02 a.m. to 2:31 p.m. and the afternoon ebb from 2:31 to 9:03 p.m. The 8:02 low is slack, dead water at the inlet, and the first hour of the flood after it is the topwater window Native Son and Carolina Flats both described on Sept. 1, a walking plug along the grass edges and over the oyster bars at docks and creek mouths; as the water tops the bars toward 2:31 p.m., a gold spoon or a paddletail on a quarter-ounce head in the flooded grass, and a live shrimp under a popping cork over the deeper oyster edges and ledges for the trout Speckled Specialist had scattered but of good size, with the gentler neap flood meaning the bait moves less and the cork gets worked slower over the same edges.
