Burrow throws for 409 yards and 4 touchdowns as Bengals beat Steelers 38-18 for the AFC North lead

Joe Burrow completed 35 of 44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns before Joe Flacco replaced him midway through the fourth quarter, and the Bengals beat Pittsburgh 38-18 at Paycor Stadium on Sunday night to take sole possession of first place in the AFC North at 7-2, per ESPN's box score and AP's game story. The line score was Cincinnati 3, 21, 7, 7 against Pittsburgh 0, 3, 0, 15: Evan McPherson's 24-yard field goal, Burrow's touchdown passes to Drew Sample (3 yards), Chase Brown (2 and 4 yards) and Dohnte Meyers (15 yards) with nine seconds left in the half, and Samaje Perine's 1-yard run, against Chris Boswell's 45-yard field goal and Aaron Rodgers' fourth-quarter touchdown passes to DK Metcalf and Pat Freiermuth. Ja'Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards; Rodgers was 15 of 29 for 143 yards with three sacks and no completion until his eighth attempt, and Jaylen Warren was held to 15 yards on seven carries. Cincinnati had 484 yards and 30 first downs to Pittsburgh's 222 and 17, has won five straight for its best start since 2015, and is a week removed from its win over Atlanta in Madrid. Guard Dalton Risner (left knee) and linebacker Oren Burks (groin) left the game; the Steelers lost safety DeShon Elliott to a knee injury in warmups and left guard Mason McCormick to a right ankle injury. Pittsburgh is 6-3. The Bengals play at Washington on Monday, Nov. 23, at 8:15 p.m. ET, outside this week's window.

Hoshoryu drops to a 5th loss, the most for a yokozuna through 9 days since 2017

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu was pushed out by sekiwake Atamifuji on Monday in Fukuoka, his second straight loss, and stands at four wins and five losses after day 9 of the Kyushu basho (tournament); Nikkan Sports says it is the first time a yokozuna has reached five losses by day 9, forfeits excluded, since Kisenosato at the 2017 Kyushu tournament, and that with at most 10 wins left to him, and no top-division yusho (championship) won with 10 or fewer wins since the six-tournament calendar began in 1958, he is likely to miss the Emperor's Cup for an 11th tournament since his promotion. Sponichi's account has Hoshoryu driving Atamifuji, at 198 kilograms the heaviest man in the division, to the edge with a right inside grip before pulling the arm out for a shoulder-slap attempt and stepping out as Atamifuji turned him; he did not speak to reporters afterward. His stablemaster Tatsunami said his ring sense is not yet back after three tournaments out and that he could only respect the wrestler's wish to compete, and JSA chairman Hakkaku said Hoshoryu has no choice but to keep wrestling desperately and get his head in on the charge. He faces Takerufuji, whom he has beaten three times without a loss, on day 10. Separately, Nikkan Sports reported that maegashira 6 Kotoeiho, out since day 3 with a herniated cervical disc, returns on day 10 against Daieisho.

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 93.8 cfs, 1.6 feet at 5:15 a.m., falling on both flow and stage against 139 cfs and 1.83 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 94 cfs - prime wading water. The fetcher reads the gauge at Dyer as falling, and Sunday's paper carried 106 cfs and 1.67 feet at the same hour, so the river is down about an eighth on flow and down seven hundredths of a foot on stage in 24 hours: the bump that crested Saturday is nearly spent, and the 139 cfs the fetcher's comparison names is Saturday's reading, not Sunday's, the 48-hour quirk again. At 94 cfs the Williams is back to a long-leader, small-fly problem: low, clearing water, the fish back on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and with the cold nights and short days the midday hours fish best. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a long leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, with a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water; a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks is a first-hour and last-hour play now that the colour is gone.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.03 feet at 5 a.m., steady against 24.86 in the fetcher's comparison. 25.0 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with ordinary bank access, a hundredth of a foot above Sunday's printed reading and steady. The wing dams and the riprap are still showing rock and the water is clear enough to fish structure rather than the slack behind it: shad are the forage, so a white or shad-pattern swimbait or a lipless crankbait worked down the face of the wing dams and along the riprap is the pattern, slowed down for water that read frost inland overnight, with a jig and crawfish on the slack side of the lock wall for the fish holding off the current. The R.C. Byrd tailwater itself is not gauged; this is the pool side.USGS 03201500 (Ohio River at Point Pleasant); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.56 feet at 5 a.m., rising against 25.91 in the fetcher's comparison. 26.6 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool and rising, a third of a foot above Sunday's printed reading and the only one of the three gauges moving up, which at this stage is the dam passing water rather than weather. Below the dam the pattern is the structure the dam makes: skipjack and shad stack in the current below the gates, and a spoon or a heavy jig worked in the churn, or a live skipjack drifted along the current seams off the lock wall, is the hybrid and catfish setup, with a little more current to work with than Sunday; the riprap along the Huntington bank fishes a crawfish-colour jig on the slack side where the bank current breaks.
