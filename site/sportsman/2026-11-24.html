Commanders beat the Bengals 22-20 on a field goal as time expired, ending Cincinnati's five-game run

Washington defeated Cincinnati 22-20 at Northwest Stadium in Landover on Monday night before 64,101, per ESPN's box score and the AP game story. Evan McPherson's 56-yard field goal with 46 seconds left had put the Bengals ahead 20-19, but Jayden Daniels, with no timeouts, hit rookie Antonio Williams for 22 and then 42 yards to reach the Cincinnati 8, and Drew Stevens kicked a 26-yard field goal as time expired, his fifth of the night, all in the second half. Joe Burrow went 28 of 40 for 300 yards and touchdowns of 31 yards to Tee Higgins and 4 to Colbie Young; Ja'Marr Chase and Higgins combined for seven catches and 74 yards against a defense that forced checkdowns, and Chase said "They did their normal, we just didn't pick it up and execute it the right way." Daniels passed for 283 yards and led Washington with 55 rushing, and Williams had nine catches for 122. Cincinnati is 7-3 and still leads the AFC North, one game ahead of Cleveland and Pittsburgh, and hosts New Orleans on Sunday at 1 p.m. ET; Washington is 4-5-1.

Spurs beat the Kings 123 to 107 without Wembanyama, Fox and Harper and stay unbeaten on the road

On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 83.8 cfs, 1.54 feet at 5:15 a.m., steady on flow against 87.0 cfs and steady on stage against 1.56 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 84 cfs - prime wading water. The gauge at Dyer reads 83.8 cfs and 1.54 feet, the fetcher's 24-hour comparison 87.0 cfs and 1.56 feet, so the river has shed three cfs on the day and both trends read steady: the fall of the past week has flattened out and the river is holding in the low 80s. At 84 cfs and clear it is a long-leader, small-fly problem, the fish on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and in late-November water the midday hours fish best once the sun is on it. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a 12-foot leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water, and a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks in the first and last hour of light. Water this thin and this clear asks for a longer cast, a finer tippet and an approach from downstream; with rain due Wednesday night the flat line is the day to fish it.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.82 feet at 5 a.m., rising against 24.53 in the fetcher's comparison. 24.8 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with ordinary bank access, up about three tenths of a foot on the fetcher's 24-hour comparison and reading rising, the second morning the upper gauge has come up after a week of falling. The wing dams and the riprap are still showing rock and the water is clear enough to fish structure rather than the slack behind it: shad are the forage, so a white or shad-pattern swimbait or a lipless crankbait worked down the face of the wing dams and along the riprap is the pattern, slowed down for water that has seen a run of cold nights, with a jig and crawfish trailer on the slack side of the lock wall for the fish holding off the current. With the pool nudging up rather than falling, the fish spread back along the length of each dam instead of bunching at the channel end, so the whole face is worth covering. The R.C.
