West Virginia

  • Williams River — 54.9 cfs, 1.34 feet, rising. 55 cfs - prime wading water. Up from 44.2 cfs at this hour Friday, and the stage is nine hundredths higher at 1.34 feet: Friday's showers put a small bump in the river overnight, the first upward move in a week of falling water. It is still low, still wadeable everywhere, and a bump this size on a freestone stream is a good thing, a little color off the banks and a reason for fish that had been pinned in the plunge pools to slide back into the pocket water between them. That makes this morning less of a stalking problem than Friday was: a 9-foot leader to 5X will do instead of the 12-footer, a size 14 or 16 caddis pupa or a soft-hackle swung through the pockets on the first of the rise, a size 14 black stonefly nymph dead-drifted down the seams into each pool, and an olive sculpin streamer bumped along the undercut banks while the light is low and the water carries any tint. Caddis, stoneflies and sculpin are the forage. The window is the morning. NWS Charleston's forecast for Cowen, generated 5:39 a.m., has showers and storms likely from 1 p.m. on with a 60% chance, then 70% tonight with a quarter to half an inch, and a flood watch is in effect for Webster County from noon today until 1 a.m. Sunday; the 5:29 a.m. discussion has precipitable water near 2 inches and light steering flow, a renewed flash-flood threat, and the watch widened a tier of counties eastward on the terrain. A half inch on ground that took Friday's rain moves a freestone river fast; be off the water when the storms arrive.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.69 feet, falling. 24.7 ft and falling — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Down 0.99 feet from 25.68 at this hour Friday: the Kanawha and Mason County creek water from Thursday's rain has passed through the pool and the gauge is back near where it sat Thursday. That is a clearing river at normal pool, and a clearing river fishes structure, not slack. Shad are the forage, so the first stop is the current side of the wing-dam tips and the riprap on the outside bends with a white or shad-pattern swimbait on a half-ounce head and a shad-colored square-bill crankbait knocked along the rock; keep the chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait for the creek mouths, where the last of the color hangs, and a black-and-blue jig for the barge-cut edges. Fish it early. NWS Charleston's forecast for Point Pleasant, generated 5:39 a.m., has fog until 10 a.m., showers and storms likely by late afternoon with a 60% chance and a tenth to a quarter inch, then 90% tonight with a quarter to half an inch, and a flood watch runs for Mason County from noon until 1 a.m. Sunday; the 5:29 a.m. discussion puts the greatest flash-flood risk in the Mid-Ohio Valley, where soils are wet from this week's rain and the high-resolution ensembles give better than a 50% chance of more than 3 inches in spots. The creeks will color the pool again tonight if that verifies.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.59 feet, rising. 26.6 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Down 1.95 feet from 28.54 at this hour Friday. The crest came and went: the fetcher's trend flag reads rising against its own 24-hour comparison, but the NWPS observed trace, read at 5:40 a.m., has the gauge between 26.59 and 26.79 feet from 1:15 to 5 a.m., which is a river holding flat at normal pool, not one climbing.
