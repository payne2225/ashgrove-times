Manchester United sack Carrick and move to hire Pochettino from the USMNT

Manchester United confirmed Thursday morning that Michael Carrick has departed as men's first-team head coach, after he met chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox at Carrington following their Wednesday meeting with Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco; the club statement said it intends to appoint a permanent successor as quickly as possible, and ESPN reported, citing sources, that United are close to an agreement with U.S. men's national team coach Mauricio Pochettino, have made contact with U.S. Soccer ahead of triggering his $10 million release clause, and would pay about $2 million more for his staff, including assistant Jesus Perez. Carrick, 45, who signed a two-year contract in May after winning 12 of 17 matches to finish third last season, leaves with United 18th in the Premier League on one league win all season and three wins in 15 matches in all competitions, his last match a home draw with Aston Villa in which United twice came back from two goals down; sources told ESPN that U.S. Soccer expects Pochettino to oversee both Concacaf Nations League quarterfinal legs against Haiti, Saturday in Miami at 4:30 p.m. ET and Tuesday in Cincinnati at 7 p.m. ET, and United's next match is at Anfield on Nov. 22.

Monken says Watson starts over Sanders on the 'full body of work'

Fujiseiun takes a first kinboshi as Hoshoryu loses a 3rd straight bout

Maegashira (rank-and-file) 2 Fujiseiun, 28, got his favored right-hand inside grip off the tachiai (initial charge), denied yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu any hold on his belt and forced him out on day 5 at the Fukuoka Kokusai Center on Thursday for his first kinboshi (gold star, a rank-and-filer's win over a yokozuna), a day after losing his first bout against a yokozuna to Onosato; Hoshoryu, 27, back from three tournaments out after right knee surgery, has given up a kinboshi on three straight days, to Fujiryoga, Ura and Fujiseiun, which Kyodo said he last did at the 2025 Nagoya basho (tournament), and has two wins and three losses after opening with two wins. Japan Sumo Association chairman Hakkaku said the yokozuna's legs had no strength in them, that consecutive losses are tough and that a yokozuna has to rebuild himself and always go forward; chief judge Kokonoe, the former ozeki (champion) Chiyotaikai, said Hoshoryu's sumo looked brittle bout by bout, that the knee did not look bad, and that he wants the kihaku issen (a flash of fighting spirit) Hoshoryu pledged at his promotion. Fujiseiun, who went from Meiji University to a corporate job before turning to sumo during the pandemic and reached the top division in March, said his mind went blank at the win and that he had felt frustration, not congratulation, when his younger stablemate Fujiryoga took his own first kinboshi on day 3.
