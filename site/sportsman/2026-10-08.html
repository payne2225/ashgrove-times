Water 73.4F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, observed 5:36 a.m. Moon waning crescent, 10% lit, 26.6 days old. Fish the moving water either side of high at 6:50 AM and 7:16 PM in the sound. Two days short of the new moon, 26.6 days old and 10 percent lit, the moon is a sliver and the springs are nearly here: the U.S. Naval Observatory's phase table, which answered again this morning, puts the new moon Saturday, Oct. 10, at 15:50 UT, 11:50 a.m. ET, with the first quarter Oct. 18 and the full moon Oct. 26, so the swings grow through the weekend and the inlet current with them. The sound at Hampstead came off a 0.5-foot low at 1:01 a.m. and fills 3.6 feet to a 4.1-foot high at 6:50 a.m., slack high a little after first light; it then drains the same 3.6 feet to a 0.5-foot low at 1:17 p.m., so the last two hours of the morning ebb, about 11:15 a.m. to 1:17, land in full daylight, and fills 3.7 feet in six hours to the day's big water, a 4.2-foot high at 7:16 p.m., about dusk, with the evening ebb running on into the night; all four sound events fall inside Thursday. The surf at Ocean City Beach pier runs ahead, and its first event belongs to Wednesday: low 11:58 p.m. Wednesday at 0.3, high 6:11 a.m. at 5.0, low 12:19 p.m. at 0.2, high 6:34 p.m. at 5.1; today's lags from the two tables are 39 and 42 minutes on the highs and 63 and 58 minutes on the lows, the lows again lagging longer, the long-ebb pattern. For the crew's water two miles north of the inlet, tide stage first: the red drum window is the bottom of the morning ebb, roughly 11:15 a.m. to 1:17 p.m., in daylight, and again on the evening ebb after 7:16 p.m. in the dark; the flood from 1:17 to 7:16 covers the oyster bars and grass edges and puts the drum in the flooded grass and under the docks around the 7:16 p.m. high, the highest water of the day and the last of the light; slack at 6:50 a.m., 1:17 p.m. and 7:16 p.m. is dead water. The water at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, read 73.4 degrees at 5:36 a.m., the same figure as Wednesday's 5:36 a.m. reading, so the three-morning slide from Sunday's 79.5 has paused for a day. What is running is a week old, not a month: the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry October report posted Thursday, Oct. 1, at 9 a.m. ET, seven days ago, and the feed carried it as its newest item at 5:51 this morning. East Coast Sports has slot red drum with over-slots mixed in off the surf and piers, Spanish and bluefish plentiful, kings starting to show at the piers, and inshore red drum as the main target, scattered through the ICW, the creeks and the inlets, with no real word on trout; Native Son has mullet everywhere and reds at the docks, creek mouths and grass lines, a few trout on early topwater, glass minnows thick off the beach; Spring Tide has the reds schooled in the bays at 22 to 28 inches and better, topwater some days and cut bait others, trout quiet until it cools, and Spanish pushing inside the inlet on a high tide; Speckled Specialist has reds on Carolina-rigged live mullet, trout in the first hour of light on a Spook Jr. then live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet, and 3- to 4-pound black drum on the bridges and oyster reefs with shrimp; Carolina Flats has big mullet in the ICW, reds on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks and flounder as bycatch; Plan 9 has 14- to 22-inch Spanish and false albacore in 15 to 20 feet just outside the inlet on small metal and epoxy jigs; South End Adventures has the bigger Spanish over hard bottom in 45 to 50 feet under the birds. Off the piers, Jolly Roger reports kings to 32 pounds on the bait, Spanish getting bigger, and night bottom fishing for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish, black drum and some flounder; Surf City reports good-sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot and occasional over-slot reds, and evening mixed bags of black drum, mullet, spots and croakers; Seaview reports the mullet migration starting, Spanish and blues on Gotchas or diamond jigs, and flounder, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, trout and black drum on the bottom. Coastal Angler's Topsail column is still Sept. 1, 37 days old. Setups grounded in that and today's tide: first light comes on the last of the flood toward the 6:50 a.m. slack high, so a Spook Jr. or another walking topwater over the flooded grass edges and the dock shadows for the trout in the first hour, and keep it going as the ebb gets started; as the ebb runs out toward 1:17, and especially from 11:15 on, cut mullet or a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig fan-cast a couple of feet off the creek-mouth channel edges for the drum, and a live shrimp under a slip cork in the deeper ICWW channel for the trout; on the six-hour flood from 1:17 to 7:16, a Carolina-rigged live finger mullet, the bait the whole report is built on, along the grass edges and across the oyster bars as they cover, or a Gulp shrimp or a three-inch paddletail under a popping cork drifted along the edges, and a Gotcha plug or small metal jig inside the inlet if the Spanish come in on the high as Spring Tide describes; at the 7:16 p.m. high, the day's highest water and the last of the light, a weedless gold spoon or a soft plastic on a weighted swimbait hook in the flooded grass and under the docks; on the evening ebb after dark, Carolina-rigged cut mullet again off the channel edges for the drum, and a half-ounce bucktail with a Gulp trailer bounced through the channel edges for the flounder, which are release only under proclamation FF-27-2026; and at the dock lights, with a 10 percent moon giving them almost no competition, a small white paddletail or a live shrimp under a float for the trout, with a fiddler crab or a piece of shrimp tight to the bridge pilings and oyster rocks on the moving water for the black drum and sheepshead. On the beach, the Spanish on the flooding surf from the 12:19 p.m. low toward the 6:34 p.m. high take Gotcha plugs and small metal or epoxy jigs under the birds, with the glass minnows the shops describe as the forage and false albacore mixed in outside the inlet.
