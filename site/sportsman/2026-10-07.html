Water 73.4F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, observed 5:36 a.m. Moon waning crescent, 17% lit, 25.6 days old. Fish the moving water either side of high at 5:55 AM and 6:27 PM in the sound. Three days short of the new moon, 25.6 days old and 17 percent lit, the moon is at the thin end of the waning crescent and the springs are building: the U.S. Naval Observatory's table, which answered again this morning, puts the new moon Saturday, Oct. 10, at 15:50 UT, 11:50 a.m. ET, after the last quarter Oct. 3, with the first quarter Oct. 18 and the full moon Oct. 26, so the swings grow every day through the weekend and the inlet current with them. The sound at Hampstead came off a 0.6-foot low at 12:13 a.m. and fills 3.3 feet to a 3.9-foot high at 5:55 a.m., slack high right at first light; it then drains 3.5 feet to a 0.4-foot low at 12:22 p.m., so the last two hours of the morning ebb, about 10:15 a.m. to 12:22, land in full daylight, and fills 3.9 feet in six hours to the day's big water, a 4.3-foot high at 6:27 p.m., with the evening ebb running on into the night; all four events fall inside Wednesday and nothing spills past midnight. The surf at Ocean City Beach pier runs ahead: high 5:20 a.m. at 4.7, low 11:23 a.m. at 0.3, high 5:46 p.m. at 5.2, low 11:58 p.m. at 0.3; today's lags from the two tables are 35 and 41 minutes on the highs and 59 minutes on the midday low, the low again lagging longer, the long-ebb pattern. For the crew's water two miles north of the inlet, tide stage first: the red drum window is the bottom of the morning ebb, roughly 10:15 a.m. to 12:22 p.m., in daylight, and again on the evening ebb after 6:27 p.m. in the dark; the flood from 12:22 to 6:27 covers the oyster bars and grass edges and puts the drum in the flooded grass and under the docks around the 6:27 p.m. high, the highest water of the day and the last of the light; slack at 5:55 a.m., 12:22 p.m. and 6:27 p.m. is dead water. The water at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast, read 73.4 degrees at 5:36 a.m., 2.7 under Tuesday's 76.1, 4.7 under Monday's 78.1 and 6.1 under Sunday's 79.5, the third step down in three mornings and the cooling the shops say the trout are waiting on. What is running is six days old, not a month: the Fisherman's Post Topsail and Sneads Ferry October report posted Thursday, Oct. 1, at 9 a.m. ET, and the feed carried it at 5:50 this morning. East Coast Sports has slot red drum with over-slots mixed in off the surf and piers, Spanish and bluefish plentiful, kings starting to show at the piers, and inshore red drum as the main target, scattered through the ICW, the creeks and the inlets, with no real word on trout; Native Son has mullet everywhere and reds at the docks, creek mouths and grass lines, a few trout on early topwater, glass minnows thick off the beach; Spring Tide has the reds schooled in the bays at 22 to 28 inches and better, topwater some days and cut bait others, trout quiet until it cools, and Spanish pushing inside the inlet on a high tide; Speckled Specialist has reds on Carolina-rigged live mullet, trout in the first hour of light on a Spook Jr. then live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet, and 3- to 4-pound black drum on the bridges and oyster reefs with shrimp; Carolina Flats has big mullet in the ICW, reds on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks and flounder as bycatch; Plan 9 has 14- to 22-inch Spanish and false albacore in 15 to 20 feet just outside the inlet on small metal and epoxy jigs; South End Adventures has the bigger Spanish over hard bottom in 45 to 50 feet under the birds. Off the piers, Jolly Roger reports kings to 32 pounds on the bait, Spanish getting bigger, and night bottom fishing for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish, black drum and some flounder; Surf City reports good-sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot and occasional over-slot reds, and evening mixed bags of black drum, mullet, spots and croakers; Seaview reports the mullet migration starting, Spanish and blues on Gotchas or diamond jigs, and flounder, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, trout and black drum on the bottom. Coastal Angler's Topsail column is still Sept. 1, 36 days old. Setups grounded in that and today's tide: first light comes on the slack high at 5:55 a.m. with the water nearly three degrees cooler at Beaufort than Tuesday, so a Spook Jr. or another walking topwater over the flooded grass edges and the dock shadows for the trout in the first hour, as the ebb gets going; as the ebb runs out toward 12:22, and especially from 10:15 on, cut mullet or a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig fan-cast a couple of feet off the creek-mouth channel edges for the drum, and a live shrimp under a slip cork in the deeper ICWW channel for the trout once the sun is up; on the six-hour flood from 12:22 to 6:27, a Carolina-rigged live finger mullet, the bait the whole report is built on, along the grass edges and across the oyster bars as they cover, or a Gulp shrimp or a three-inch paddletail under a popping cork drifted along the edges, and a Gotcha plug or small metal jig inside the inlet if the Spanish come in on the high as Spring Tide describes; at the 6:27 p.m. high, the day's highest water and the last of the light, a weedless gold spoon or a soft plastic on a weighted swimbait hook in the flooded grass and under the docks; on the evening ebb after dark, Carolina-rigged cut mullet again off the channel edges for the drum, and a half-ounce bucktail with a Gulp trailer bounced through the channel edges for the flounder, which are release only; and at the dock lights, a small white paddletail or a live shrimp under a float for the trout, with a fiddler crab or a piece of shrimp tight to the bridge pilings and oyster rocks on the moving water for the black drum and sheepshead. On the beach, the Spanish on the flooding surf from the 11:23 a.m. low toward the 5:46 p.m. high take Gotcha plugs and small metal or epoxy jigs under the birds, with the glass minnows the shops describe as the forage and false albacore mixed in outside the inlet.
