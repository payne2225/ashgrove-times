West Virginia women fall to No. 13 Notre Dame 78-64 at the Battle 4 Atlantis

Notre Dame defeated No. 22 West Virginia 78-64 at Imperial Arena on Paradise Island, Bahamas, on Thursday before 326, per ESPN's box score and the AP game story. JSA says seven sekitori will miss the winter tour as Hatsu basho sits 44 days out

The Japan Sumo Association said Thursday that seven sekitori (salaried wrestlers in the top two divisions) will miss the winter jungyo (regional tour), which opens Sunday in Amakusa, Kumamoto Prefecture, and runs through Dec. 20 in Uruma, Okinawa, Nikkan Sports reported: six makuuchi (top-division) men including Takayasu, Kotoeiho, Shishi and Ichiyamamoto, and one from juryo (second division). No one ranked sanyaku (the three ranks below yokozuna) or higher is out, and yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu, nine wins and six losses in Fukuoka, joins from the first day; six juryo men including Kagayaki and Shodai fill the places. The JSA's schedule page lists the banzuke (rankings) for the Hatsu basho (New Year tournament) for release Dec. 21, with the tournament opening Jan. 10 at the Kokugikan in Tokyo, 44 days out, and closing Jan. 24.

  • Williams River — 644 cfs, 3.2 feet at 5:15 a.m., rising on both flow and stage against 74.4 cfs and 1.48 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 644 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. The gauge at Dyer reads 644 cfs and 3.2 feet at 5:15 a.m., against 74.4 cfs and 1.48 feet 24 hours earlier, and both trends read rising: the river has come up nearly ninefold on flow and 1.72 feet on stage overnight, from the 71 cfs the fetcher called prime wading water on Thanksgiving morning. The fetcher's verdict is the paper's: 644 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. At this flow and still rising the pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks the config records are one brown current, the seams are gone, and there is no wading setup for the Williams today; what the weekend holds depends on the gauge turning over and falling, which this desk does not forecast. From the bank, the one pattern the water's own structure supports is a large dark sculpin streamer on a sink tip, swung into the slack of the inside bends and the eddies below the plunge pools where fish pushed off their lies by the surge hold out of the current, with sculpin the forage that a rising, coloured river puts on the move; it is a bank cast, not a wade. The gauge, not the calendar, decides when the long-leader small-fly water comes back.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.15 feet at 5 a.m., rising against 24.76 in the fetcher's comparison. 25.1 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. This is the upstream bracket, the pool side, 14 miles above the dam.
