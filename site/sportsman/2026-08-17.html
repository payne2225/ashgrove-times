North Carolina

  • Prime · Red drum — Coastal waters: 18-inch minimum and 27-inch maximum total length, one per day. It is unlawful to possess a red drum over 27 inches.NCDMF
  • Prime · Spotted seatrout — Coastal waters: 14 to 20 inches total length, three per day, plus one fish over 26 inches.NCDMF
  • Prime · Sheepshead — Coastal waters: 14-inch minimum total length, five per day.NCDMF
  • Bluefish is five per day from a for-hire vessel but three per day for private recreational anglers, which is the number that applies to most of this readership.NCDMF
  • Flounder is closed in North Carolina coastal waters and unlawful to possess. Topsail Sound is coastal water, so Marine Fisheries governs it, not the inland digest.NCDMF
On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 4620 cfs and rising, 7.55 feet. 4620 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. Up from 179 cfs and 2.00 feet a day ago, so nothing is fishable and nothing is wadeable. The pocket water and plunge pools that are the whole river in August are gone under. As it drops the seams along the undercut banks clear first, and that is where a heavy stonefly nymph and a sculpin pattern go in.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 27.96 feet and rising. 28.0 ft and rising - up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up nearly three feet in a day and coloured, so the fish are off the main current. Shad are the forage: work a white swimbait through the slack behind the wing dams and the barge cuts rather than the open channel.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 30.75 feet and rising. 30.8 ft and rising - up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Also up nearly three feet. The lock wall and the riprap are the holding water while it is pushing; a crawfish-coloured crankbait tight to the rock is the second look after the swimbait.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 12:08 AM and 12:43 PM at Hampstead; lows 6:32 AM and 7:05 PM. Fish the moving water either side of high at 12:08 AM and 12:43 PM in the sound. Tide first: the 12:43 p.m. high is the one daylight window, and the push into it and the fall behind it are the hours. Mud minnows, shrimp and finger mullet are the forage, so work the grass edges and the oyster bars while the water is moving.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
