West Virginia

  • Williams River — 54.9 cfs, 1.34 feet, flat. 55 cfs - prime wading water. Unchanged from this hour Saturday: the gauge read 54.9 cfs and 1.34 feet at 5:15 a.m. both mornings, and the fetcher's rising flag is measuring against Friday. Saturday's flood watch did not reach this water; the two-day trace shows the river nudging to 56.2 cfs from 3 to 7 p.m. Saturday and settling back by 1 a.m., a bump of a hundredth of a foot while Braxton and Lewis counties took 3.5 to 6.5 inches. So it is the low, clear, long-leader river it has been all week, with no fresh color to hide behind. Caddis, stoneflies and sculpin are the forage, and pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks are the structure, which at 55 cfs means a 12-foot leader to 6X, a size 16 or 18 caddis dry or emerger fished upstream through the pockets before the sun is on the water, a size 14 stonefly nymph under it dead-drifted into the heads of the plunge pools, and an olive sculpin worked tight under the undercut banks only while the fog holds. NWS Charleston's forecast for Cowen, updated 5:03 a.m., keeps areas of fog and a 40% chance of showers before 11 a.m., a slight chance of storms between 11 and 5, a high near 77 with a west wind around 5, then a 50% chance of showers after midnight with under a tenth of an inch and a low around 60. The fog is the window; the low water is the problem.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.65 feet, up about a foot since Saturday morning. 25.6 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Up 0.96 feet from the 24.69 this paper printed at this hour Saturday; the fetcher's steady flag compares against Friday. The two-day trace has the pool bottoming at 24.66 feet at 1 a.m. Saturday, climbing from 8 a.m. on as the Mason County creeks came in, 25.67 at 6 p.m., 25.78 at midnight, and easing to 25.65 by 5 a.m. The Weather Service's Ohio River forecast for Point Pleasant, issued 10:05 p.m. Saturday, has it back up to 26.4 by 8 a.m., 26.6 from tonight through Monday morning, then a slow fall to 25.8 by Tuesday evening; action stage is 38 feet, so this is a foot of colored creek water on a normal pool, not a flood. That water is still arriving from Mason County, where two flood warnings run to 8 a.m., so the pool fishes the slack today, not the current. Shad, skipjack and crawfish are the forage, and wing dams, riprap, the lock wall and barge cuts the structure: a black-and-blue or green-pumpkin jig with a crawfish trailer on the downstream, slack side of the wing-dam tips and the riprap points; a chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait slow-rolled through the mudlines where the creeks meet the pool, where shad stack against the color change; a white swimbait only where the water clears. NWS Charleston's forecast for Point Pleasant, updated 5:03 a.m., has fog before 10 a.m., partly sunny and a high near 86 with a west wind of 1 to 6, and a 40% chance of showers and storms from 9 p.m. to 2 a.m. with under a tenth of an inch.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 27.69 feet, rising. 27.7 ft — up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid.   • Topsail Sound — Sound lows 4:33 AM at 0.4 ft and 5:01 PM at 0.6 ft at Hampstead; highs 10:28 AM at 4.2 ft and 10:46 PM at 3.8 ft. Surf at Ocean City Beach pier: low 3:10 AM at 0.0 ft, high 9:31 AM at 5.4 ft, low 3:44 PM at 0.2 ft, high 9:49 PM at 4.7 ft. Water 84.0F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast. Moon waxing crescent, 3% lit, 1.5 days old. Fish the moving water either side of high at 10:28 AM and 10:46 PM in the sound. Spring tides: the moon is a day and a half past new, and the sound swings 3.8 feet between the 4:33 a.m. low and the 10:28 a.m. high, with 5.4 feet of range at the ocean station, which means the hardest inlet current of the month and the most bait moving through it. The sound today runs 57 minutes behind the inlet on both highs and 77 to 83 minutes behind on the lows, so the afternoon ebb in the backwater stretches from 10:28 a.m. to 5:01 p.m., and the last of it, roughly 3 to 5 p.m., is the red drum window on the tide preference that matters most on this island: shrimp and finger mullet draining out of the grass into the creek channels, and a cut mullet or a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig a couple of feet off the channel edge at a creek mouth. That window also sits under the Weather Service's moderate thunderstorm and waterspout potential and a heat index up to 101, so the earlier one is first light: dawn falls on dead low water at 4:33, useless at the inlet, but by the time there is light to see the flood is moving, and a topwater walked along the grass edges and over the oyster bars on the rising water is the trout and drum bite Carolina Flats and Native Son both described on Sept. 1. As the water tops the bars toward the 10:28 high, a gold spoon or a paddletail on a quarter-ounce head in the flooded grass, and a live shrimp under a popping cork over the deeper oyster edges. Flounder, with Monday the last day of harvest at 15 inches and one a day, want moving water against structure either way, and Carolina Flats' 1-ounce bucktail with a Gulp curly tail worked along the ICWW channel edges and dock pilings on the afternoon ebb is the report's own setup for the 20-to-24-inch class it described. After dark the flood to the 10:46 p.m. high pushes bait past the dock lights, and a small paddletail or a shrimp imitation drifted through the edge of the light is the trout cast. Surf: Spanish and bluefish want the calm early hours, and the forecast's southwest wind at 5 to 10 with a 1-to-3-foot surf allows it. Water 84.0F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
