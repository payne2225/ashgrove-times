Todd Benzinger, who caught the last out of the Reds' 1990 World Series, dies at 63

The Reds announced Thursday that Todd Benzinger, their first baseman from 1989 to 1991 and the man who squeezed the final out of the 1990 World Series sweep of Oakland, died Wednesday at 63. Benzinger played nine major league seasons, debuting with Boston in 1987 and finishing with San Francisco in 1995, managed Class A Dayton in 2009 and 2010, and remained active in youth baseball in New Richmond, Ohio, where he went to high school. Rick Walls, executive director of the Reds Hall of Fame and Museum, said Benzinger "remained close to the Cincinnati community" and was a regular at the Reds Caravan, fantasy camp and Redsfest. No cause was given.

Concacaf moves the USMNT's Nations League first leg against Haiti to Miami

Concacaf approved the Haitian federation's request Thursday to move its home leg of the Nations League quarterfinal against the United States from Kingston, Jamaica, to Nu Stadium in Miami on Nov. 14, so both legs will be played on U.S. soil, with the return in Cincinnati on Nov. 17. Haiti has not played at home since 2021 and its national stadium was taken over by gangs in 2024; Concacaf said the arrangement, which lets Haiti play designated home matches in the opponent's territory, stands for as long as the security situation persists and needs approval match by match. U.S. Soccer also confirmed Tuesday that the team will close 2026 with a friendly against Jamaica in Fort Lauderdale on Dec. 13 at 3:30 p.m. ET, a December camp replacing the usual January one.
