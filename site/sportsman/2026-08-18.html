On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 1270 cfs and rising, 4.15 feet. 1270 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. Down from Monday's crest but still four times a normal August flow and rising again on the overnight rain. Nothing here is wadeable. Watch for the number to fall back under about 300 cfs; the seams along the undercut banks clear first, and a heavy stonefly nymph or a sculpin pattern goes in there before anything else.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 30.77 feet and rising. 30.8 ft and rising - up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up more than five feet in a day and carrying colour, so the fish are off the main channel entirely. Shad are the forage: a white swimbait worked through the slack behind the wing dams and in the barge cuts is the first look.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 35.04 feet and rising. 35.0 ft and rising - high water. Banks are going under and the river is carrying debris. Up nearly eight feet in a day, which is the biggest move either gauge has made this month. The banks are the hazard rather than the fishing; when it steadies, the lock wall and the riprap hold fish and a crawfish-coloured crankbait tight to the rock is the second look after the swimbait.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 12:57 AM and 1:33 PM at Hampstead; lows 7:15 AM and 7:59 PM. Fish the moving water either side of high at 12:57 AM and 1:33 PM in the sound. Tide before lure: the 1:33 p.m. high is the only daylight window, and the push into it and the fall behind it are the hours that matter. Mud minnows, shrimp and finger mullet are the forage, so the grass edges and the oyster bars are where a soft plastic or a live bait goes while the water is moving.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
