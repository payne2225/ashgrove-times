West Virginia

  • Williams River — 90.4 cfs, 1.58 feet, rising, up from 56.2 cfs and 1.35 feet in the fetcher's comparison and still climbing at the fetch. 90 cfs - prime wading water. This is the front of a freshet, not settled water, and the safety line comes first: NWS Charleston issued a Flash Flood Warning at 4:40 a.m. for Webster, Nicholas, Braxton, Clay, Gilmer, Lewis, Calhoun and Roane counties through 8:45 a.m., one of five flash flood warnings across the state at press time. The USGS two-day trace, pulled at 5:38 a.m., held between 56 and 62 cfs all of Saturday, bottomed at the 9 p.m. reading, then climbed hour by hour through the overnight rain to the 5:15 a.m. reading, the highest value in the record, with no crest yet. The fetcher's read string, 'prime wading water', is its flow-band verdict for 90 cfs and is printed as written; a river rising a third of a foot in six hours under a flash flood warning is not wading water until the gauge turns. When it does, the pattern for a rising, coloring freestone is the soft edges rather than the open pocket water: the inside of plunge-pool tailouts, the seams below boulders and the undercut banks where the current slackens. A rise dislodges stonefly and caddis larvae and washes sculpin off the bottom, so the forage on a rise is big, dark and low, a sculpin pattern or a stonefly nymph worked along the undercut banks and pool edges rather than a dry caddis on the riffles. The reading to watch is the crest.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.56 feet, rising, up from 25.08 in the fetcher's comparison. 25.6 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with a slow rise, and the direction is the trace's as well as the fetcher's this morning. Action stage is 38 feet, so this is ordinary bank water with fresh runoff arriving from the tributaries after the overnight storms; the flash flood warnings at press time cover Mason, Putnam, Kanawha and Jackson counties, so the Kanawha side of the pool is where the first color will show, and the riprap and wing dams upstream toward Byrd stay clearer longer. At normal pool the structure that concentrates fish is the current break: the downstream eddies of the wing dams, the riprap seams and the lock wall, where shad and skipjack stack in the slack water and crawfish work the rock, so a white swimbait or a chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait across the wing dam faces and off their tips, and a black-and-blue jig with a crawfish trailer dragged down the riprap points. A morning under active warnings is a morning to watch the tributary mouths for debris before it is a morning to fish them.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.31 feet, rising, up from 26.02 in the fetcher's comparison. 26.3 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Rising slowly below the dam, and the Weather Service's forecast for Huntington, issued 10:18 a.m. Saturday before the overnight rain, has the river at 26.6 feet Saturday evening, a crest of 27.1 feet about 8 a.m. Tuesday, Sept. 22, and 26.1 by Thursday, with no flooding category; action stage is 48 feet, so the crest is roughly 21 feet under it. The forecast predates the storms, and the Point Pleasant pool above the dam is putting on water that will show here later today. With the stage rising slowly and the pool a foot under normal high-water marks, the river fishes structure: shad and skipjack are the forage, and the seams below the lock wall and off the wing dam tips are where a slow rise concentrates them, so a three-quarter-ounce white swimbait or a skipjack-pattern jerkbait along the seam where the eddy meets the flow for hybrids and white bass, and a crawfish-colored football jig dragged down the riprap. Cabell and Wayne counties are under a flash flood warning until 9:30 a.m.; the bank below the dam is not the place to be while it runs.USGS 03206000
