West Virginia

  • Williams River — 189.0 cfs, 2.04 feet, falling off a Sunday-evening crest. 189 cfs - prime wading water. The river more than tripled since this hour Sunday: the paper printed 54.9 cfs and 1.34 feet at 5:15 a.m. Sunday, and this morning the fetcher's 24-hour comparison, which usually reaches back two days, happens to land on the same numbers, so it is right for once. The USGS two-day trace, pulled at 5:38 a.m., has the Sunday-afternoon rain arriving all at once: 68.5 cfs at 1:45 p.m., 93.8 at 2:45, 106 at 3:45, 254 at 4:45, a crest of 270 cfs and 2.32 feet at 5:30 p.m. Sunday, then a steady recession, 230 at 11:45 p.m., 205 at 2:45 a.m., 191 at 4:45 and 189.0 at 5:15. It has crested and is dropping about six to eight cfs an hour, not climbing. At 189 cfs and 2.04 feet, seven-tenths of a foot over the week's base, this freestone is wadeable at the edges and the tailouts but not the pocket water in the middle of the run, and it is carrying the first color it has had all month. Caddis, stoneflies and sculpin are the forage, and pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks the structure, which at this flow means a size 10 or 12 stonefly nymph under a split shot fished on a short line into the heads of the plunge pools and the soft seams beside the pockets, a size 14 caddis pupa as the dropper, and an olive or black sculpin streamer swung under the undercut banks where the color hides it. NWS Charleston's forecast for Cowen, updated 2:35 a.m., is partly sunny with a high near 71 and a north wind around 5, then mostly clear with a low around 51 tonight, no rain, so the drop continues.USGS 03186500

The Weather Service's forecast for Point Pleasant, issued 8:41 p.m. Sunday, was revised down from Saturday night's 26.6 crest: it now holds 25.5 by 8 a.m., 25.6 this afternoon and evening, and a slow slide to 24.6 by Friday, with the 5 a.m. observation already at the crest line; action stage is 38 feet, so this is fresh creek color on a normal pool. Shad, skipjack and crawfish are the forage, and wing dams, riprap, the lock wall and barge cuts the structure: with the mudlines from the Mason County creeks meeting the main pool this morning, a chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait slow-rolled along the color change where shad stack, a black-and-blue jig with a crawfish trailer on the downstream side of the wing-dam tips and riprap points, and a white swimbait on the outside edges where the water is clearer.
