On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 750.0 cfs, 3.39 feet at 5:15 a.m., flagged rising against 58.8 cfs and 1.37 feet in the fetcher's comparison. 750 cfs - blown out. Stay on the bank. Blown out and receding. The USGS two-day trace, pulled at 5:38 a.m., puts the crest at 4 p.m. Sunday, 1,560 cfs and 4.52 feet, after a climb from 90 cfs at dawn Sunday through the day's rain; the river has fallen every half hour since, 950 cfs at midnight, 840 at 2 a.m., 773 at 4 a.m., 755 at 5 a.m. and 750 at 5:15, so the gauge is coming down, not going up, and the rate of fall is slowing. The fetcher's 'rising' flag compares against 58.8 cfs, the first point of the two-day window at 5:45 a.m. Saturday; against the true 24-hour mark, 90.4 cfs and 1.58 feet at 5:15 a.m. Sunday, the river is also up, by 660 cfs, so the flag is right about the day and wrong about the morning. The read string is the fetcher's flow-band verdict and stands: at 750 cfs on a freestone this size the pocket water is a single sheet, the plunge pools are brown, the undercut banks are under the river rather than beside it, and the line is the bank. What the recession looks like, from the pattern and not a forecast: last week's event crested on a Sunday evening and the gauge was back at its 56-to-59 cfs base by Thursday and Friday, and this crest is about twice as high, so the heavy-nymph-on-the-seam water in the 300-to-500 band is a day or two away and the 100-cfs long-leader water is the back half of the week at the earliest, if the Flood Watch that runs until noon today over Webster and Nicholas counties does not add to it. When it does drop into the seams, the forage after a flush is big and low: stonefly nymphs and sculpin washed off the bottom, fished on the inside edges of the plunge-pool tailouts and along the undercut banks where the current first slackens, before anything on top.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.97 feet at 5 a.m., rising, up from 25.12 in the fetcher's comparison. 26.0 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Normal pool with a fast rise on, and the direction is the trace's as well as the fetcher's: the two-day record bumped to 26.45 feet at 1:45 p.m. Sunday on the first of the rain, drained back to 25.13 by 1 a.m., then turned at 2:45 a.m. and put on seven-tenths in the next two and a quarter hours to the 5 a.m. reading, the steepest climb in the record. Against the true 24-hour mark, 25.56 at 5 a.m. Sunday, it is up 0.41. That water is coming out of the tributaries under active warnings: NWS Charleston has a Flood Warning until 10:45 a.m. on north central Mason County with 1.5 to 2.5 inches down and 1 to 3 more possible, two more warnings on Jackson and Wood counties until 11:15 and 12:30, and a Flood Watch until noon on Mason, Jackson, Putnam, Kanawha and Cabell. The Kanawha side of the pool will carry the first color and debris; the riprap and wing dams upstream toward Byrd stay clearer longer. On a pool coming up this fast the fish are not on the open faces, they are in the slack: the downstream eddies of the wing dams, the inside of the riprap points and the mouths of the barge cuts, where shad and skipjack pushed off the main flow stack up. A white swimbait or a chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait worked slowly through the eddy behind the wing dam tips is the shad pattern, and a black-and-blue jig with a crawfish trailer dragged down the riprap is the rock pattern. A bank under a Flood Warning is watched from the truck until the creek mouths stop running brown.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 28.53 feet at 5 a.m., rising, up from 26.53 in the fetcher's comparison. 28.5 ft and rising — up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up two feet on the day and pushing, with more coming. The two-day trace climbed all of Sunday from 26.31 at 5 a.m. to a bump of 28.83 at 10:15 p.m., then held between 28.5 and 28.7 through the night and stood at 28.53 at 5 a.m., so at the fetch the gauge is flat to easing off that overnight high while the pool above the dam puts on water that will arrive here later today. The Weather Service's NWPS forecast for Huntington, issued 11:13 a.m. Sunday before most of the rain, carries the river to 30.4 feet by 2 p.m. Monday and a crest of 30.8 feet about 2 a.m. Tuesday, Sept. 22, easing to 29.1 by Wednesday afternoon and 27 by Thursday, with no flooding category; action stage is 48 feet, so the crest is 17 feet under it. That forecast predates last night's totals on Mason County and may be low. At 28.5 and pushing the river fishes the slack behind anything solid, as the fetcher says: the eddy below the lock wall, the downstream sides of the wing dams, the riprap pockets and the inside of the barge cuts, where shad and skipjack get out of the current. A three-quarter-ounce white swimbait or a skipjack-pattern jerkbait held on the seam where the eddy meets the flow is the pattern for hybrids and white bass on a rise, and a crawfish-colored football jig or a big live shad on a bottom rig in the slack pockets for the catfish that a rising, coloring river turns on. Cabell County is under the Flood Watch until noon; the bank below the dam is not the place to be while the tributaries run.USGS 03206000

Surf at Ocean City Beach pier: high 3:58 AM at 3.4 ft, low 9:47 AM at 1.1 ft, high 4:34 PM at 4.5 ft, low 11:00 PM at 1.2 ft. Water 80.6F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast. Moon waxing gibbous, 72% lit, 9.5 days old. Fish the moving water either side of high at 4:21 AM and 5:03 PM in the sound. Springs building toward Saturday's full moon: the U.S. Naval Observatory puts the full moon at Sept. 26, 16:49 Universal Time, 12:49 p.m. Eastern, so the moon is nine and a half days past new and five days short of full, the swings are growing each day and the inlet current is hardening; the sound falls 3.2 feet from the 5:03 p.m. high to the 11:31 p.m. low, the biggest drop of the day, and the surf high at 4:34 p.m. is 4.5 feet. The sound today runs 23 minutes behind the inlet on the overnight high, 55 on the morning low, 29 on the afternoon high and 31 on the evening low, the lows lagging more than the highs as usual, which stretches the ebb at the crew's water. The September pattern here is bull red drum, the start of the spot run, Spanish and kings on the beach, trout and sea mullet on the bottom. Red drum want the last of the fall: this morning's ebb runs from the 4:21 a.m. high to the 10:42 a.m. low, so the two hours before 10:42 are the drum window on the oyster bars and grass edges where the draining marsh pushes shrimp and finger mullet into the creek channels, a cut mullet or a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig a couple of feet off the channel edge at a creek mouth, or a gold spoon along the grass; the evening fall runs from 5:03 p.m. to the 11:31 p.m. low, and the dock lights on that late ebb are the trout and shrimp pattern after dark, a small paddletail or shrimp imitation drifted through the edge of the light. Mud minnows and finger mullet on the ICWW channel edges fit the moving water either side of the lows, never the slack. Flounder are closed and unlawful to possess through Sept. 30 under the Marine Fisheries limits page: the bucktail-and-Gulp cast along the channel edges finds them, and they go back. Surf: the Weather Service's surf zone forecast for Coastal Pender, issued 3:18 a.m., has surf around 2 feet, a LOW rip current risk, light and variable winds going east around 10 in the afternoon, sunny with patchy fog early, mid 80s and water around 80, a moderate waterspout risk and a very high UV index; that is the calm-morning Spanish and bluefish setup on bait pods tight to the beach the Sept. 1 pier reports described, a Gotcha plug or a casting jig under birds off the pier ends on the 9:47 a.m. surf low and into the flood.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
