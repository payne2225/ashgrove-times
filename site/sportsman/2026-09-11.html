Around the Leagues
Hoshoryu withdraws from the Aki basho, his third straight tournament out

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu, 27, will sit out the Aki basho (autumn tournament), which opens Sunday at Ryogoku Kokugikan, judging chief Asakayama said after Friday morning's bout-drawing meeting in Tokyo; it is his third straight kyujo (withdrawal), his 10th overall and his first from day one since his January 2018 debut. He had surgery July 29 to repair the medial collateral ligament in his right knee and took no bouts at the Sept. 4 open practice. Stablemaster Tatsunami told Jiji Press 'we could not get him to a state where he can put out his strength' and that Hoshoryu is 'badly down.' Onosato goes in as the lone yokozuna.

Sponichi Annex
Onosato opens against Daieisho; Kirishima's yokozuna run starts with Kotoshoho

The Japan Sumo Association set the first two days' torikumi (matchups) Friday: yokozuna Onosato, 26, seeking his first yusho (championship) since last September, faces komusubi Daieisho, back in the sanyaku (top ranks) after seven basho, on Sunday and Kotoshoho on Monday; ozeki (champion) Kirishima, 30, whose two 12-win runner-up finishes since regaining the rank put him in line for promotion, draws Kotoshoho on day one. Ozeki Aonishiki, the July champion, opens against Kotoeiho, ozeki Kotozakura against Takayasu, and sekiwake Atamifuji, chasing ozeki promotion, against Gonoyama. New sekiwake Fujinokawa, 21, meets Yoshinofuji.

Sankei Sports
Wakatakakage sits out a second basho after July leg surgery; Wakanosho also out

Maegashira Wakatakakage, 31, who had emergency surgery in July for compartment syndrome in his left leg and missed all of Nagoya, will miss the Aki basho from day one, the JSA said Friday; he is back on the practice floor in a mawashi (belt) doing basic drills, and a full absence at east maegashira 12 would send him down to juryo (the second division) for November. Wakanosho, 23, who left Nagoya on day seven with a bruised right thighbone, is also out. With Hoshoryu, three top-division wrestlers are on the kyujo list before the first bout.

On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 44.2 cfs, 1.25 feet, falling. 44 cfs - prime wading water. Down from 54.9 cfs at this hour Thursday, and the stage is off nine hundredths at 1.25 feet: the river is low, slow and as clear as it gets. At 44 cfs the plunge pools and the deeper pockets hold the fish and the riffles between them are ankle water, so it is a stalking problem more than a casting one, a 12-foot leader tapered to 6X or 7X, a size 18 tan caddis dry or a size 16 stonefly nymph dropped under it on the seams that feed each pool, and an olive or black sculpin pattern swung tight along the undercut banks in the first hour of light. Caddis, stoneflies and sculpin are the forage and none of it is big at this flow. NWS Charleston's grid for Cowen, updated 5:18 a.m., has showers and storms likely before 5 p.m. with a 60% chance and a tenth to a quarter inch, then 80% Saturday and Saturday night with a quarter to half an inch each period; the 5:24 a.m. discussion raised its concern for localized flash flooding Saturday, with the Weather Prediction Center at a slight risk for excessive rain across part of the area and a watch possible in later packages. Today's rain should not move the gauge; Saturday's might.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.68 feet, rising. 25.7 ft and rising — normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Up 0.96 feet from 24.72 at this hour Thursday, which is the Kanawha and the Mason County creeks arriving after Thursday morning's 3 to 4 inches; the flash flood warning for northern Mason County expired and no alert was in effect at 5:45 a.m. The pool is still within its normal band, so the bank fishing is the same, but the water is colored from the creek mouths down. Shad are the forage and they are pinned to the edges when the river runs off-color, so the lure is a white or chartreuse-and-white spinnerbait or a chatterbait run along the mudline where each creek meets the pool, and a black-and-blue jig pitched into the slack on the downstream side of the wing-dam tips rather than across their faces; the riprap on the outside bends will fish again once the color drops out. NWS Charleston's discussion has more showers today, mainly south and east of the Ohio, and slow-moving storms with heavy downpours Saturday, so the tributaries may color the pool again before it clears.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 28.54 feet, rising. 28.5 ft and rising — up and pushing. Expect colour and current; fish the slack behind anything solid. Up 2.13 feet from 26.41 at this hour Thursday, the rise the Weather Service was forecasting before the Mason County rain, and then some. NWS Huntington's Ohio River forecast, issued 4:10 p.m. Thursday and read on the NWPS API this morning, has the crest at 29.8 feet at 2 a.m. today, 29.0 by 8 a.m., 28.2 by 2 p.m. and 27.5 by 8 p.m., then 26.9 Saturday morning, 26.3 Sunday and 26.1 Monday, no flooding, with the action stage at 48 feet; the NWPS observed trace held between 28.6 and 28.7 feet from 3 to 5 a.m., so the river appears to have topped out short of the forecast number and is starting down. That makes today the high-water day and the weekend the drop. Shad, skipjack and crawfish are the forage. With current on the lock wall and the riprap, the fish sit in the eddies: a three-quarter-ounce crawfish-colored football jig or a Texas-rigged craw in the slack on the downstream side of the wing-dam tips and the barge-cut mouths, and a white or skipjack-pattern swimbait worked through the seam where the eddy meets the current for the bass and the hybrids pushing bait against it. The lock wall's face is for the falling water Saturday, not today.USGS 03206000

  • Topsail Sound — Sound lows 3:08 AM at 0.3 ft and 3:23 PM at 0.3 ft at Hampstead; highs 8:50 AM at 4.2 ft and 9:15 PM at 4.2 ft. Fish the moving water either side of high at 8:50 AM and 9:15 PM in the sound. The Fisherman's Post September report is 10 days old, published Tuesday, Sept. 1, and the feed carries nothing newer, so it is the month's pattern and not this morning's water. The moon was new at 11:27 p.m. ET Thursday, per the U.S. Naval Observatory, so these are spring tides: a 3.9-foot swing in the sound on both cycles, the hardest current of the month through the inlet and the most bait moving.
