Leeds beat Chelsea 4-1 at Stamford Bridge, their first league win there in 27 years

Leeds United defeated Chelsea 4-1 at Stamford Bridge on Saturday, their first Premier League win at the ground in 27 years, per ESPN's match report and box score. Emmanuel Emegha put Chelsea ahead in the 25th minute on his full debut, from a Morgan Rogers pass; Noah Okafor equalised three minutes later, Dominic Calvert-Lewin converted a 53rd-minute penalty conceded by Rogers, Okafor scored again in the 58th and Brenden Aaronson added a fourth in stoppage time. Leeds had 21 shots and eight on target to Chelsea's 14 and three; Moises Caicedo went off injured after 20 minutes. Chelsea drop to eighth on 16 points from 11 matches and Leeds climb to sixth on 17. Chelsea are next at Nottingham Forest on Friday at 3 p.m. ET.

Onosato wins the Kyushu basho in a playoff over Wakamotoharu after both finish 13 and 2

Yokozuna (grand champion) Onosato won the Kyushu basho (tournament) in Fukuoka on Sunday, beating maegashira (rank-and-file) 7 Wakamotoharu by watashikomi (thigh-grabbing push down) in a kettei-sen (championship playoff) after both finished the 15 days at 13 wins and two losses, per the Japan Sumo Association's tournament leaders page and the final-day results carried by Yahoo Japan Sports; Sports Hochi and Sponichi Annex report it as his second straight yusho (championship), his first back-to-back titles since his promotion to yokozuna and his first in Kyushu. In the regular final-day bouts Onosato pushed out yokozuna Hoshoryu, who finished nine and six, and Wakamotoharu beat sekiwake (third-highest rank) Atamifuji by tsukiotoshi (thrust down) after Atamifuji, who had him at the edge, fell first; Atamifuji ends 11 and four with Kinbozan, who forced out maegashira 1 Asanoyama, and Asahakuryu. Ozeki (champion) Aonishiki beat ozeki Kotozakura by kotenage (arm-lock throw) to finish seven and eight, a make-koshi (losing record) all the same, and ozeki Kirishima finished 10 and five. Jiji Press reports the sansho (special prizes) went to Atamifuji for outstanding performance, Tamawashi and Kinbozan for fighting spirit and Wakamotoharu for technique. In juryo (the second division) Wakamotoharu's younger brother Wakatakakage won the title at 14 and one, a win clear of Asahifuji. The JSA's English results and champions pages were still unposted shells at 5:52 a.m. ET; the Hatsu basho opens in January in Tokyo, dates to be confirmed from the JSA schedule.

  • Williams River — 88.7 cfs, 1.57 feet at 5:15 a.m., falling on flow against 97.3 cfs and steady on stage against 1.62 feet in the fetcher's 24-hour comparison. 89 cfs - prime wading water. The gauge at Dyer reads 88.7 cfs and 1.57 feet, the fetcher's 24-hour comparison 97.3 cfs and 1.62 feet, so the river has dropped under 90 for the first time this week and is still easing; the stage trend reads steady. At 89 cfs and clear it is a long-leader, small-fly problem, the fish on the pocket-water lies and the tailouts rather than the seams, and with another cold night behind it the midday hours fish best once the sun is on the water. A size 18 to 20 midge or a small Blue-winged Olive nymph under a soft indicator on a 12-foot leader through the slower pockets and the tailouts is the pattern, a stonefly nymph as the point fly in the deeper plunge pools where the larger fish hold in cold water, and a sculpin pattern swung tight to the undercut banks in the first and last hour of light. Water this thin and this clear asks for a longer cast, a finer tippet and an approach from downstream.USGS 03186500 (Williams River at Dyer); config.SPORTSMAN_WATERS

  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.48 feet at 5 a.m., steady against 26.24 in the fetcher's comparison. 26.5 ft — normal pool. Ordinary bank access.
