East Coast Sports has slot red drum with over-slots mixed in off the surf and piers, Spanish and bluefish plentiful, kings starting to show at the piers, and inshore red drum as the main target, scattered through the ICW, the creeks and the inlets, with no real word on trout; Native Son has mullet everywhere and reds at the docks, creek mouths and grass lines, a few trout on early topwater; Spring Tide has the reds schooled in the bays at 22 to 28 inches and better, topwater some days and cut bait others, and Spanish pushing inside the inlet on a high tide; Speckled Specialist has reds on Carolina-rigged live mullet, trout in the first hour of light on a Spook Jr. then live shrimp under slip corks in 15 to 20 feet, and 3- to 4-pound black drum on the bridges and oyster reefs with shrimp; Carolina Flats has big mullet in the ICW, reds on live finger mullet or Gulp under popping corks and flounder as bycatch; Plan 9 has 14- to 22-inch Spanish and false albacore in 15 to 20 feet just outside the inlet on small metal and epoxy jigs. Off the piers, Jolly Roger reports kings to 32 pounds on the bait, Spanish getting bigger, and night bottom fishing for red drum to 31 inches, Virginia mullet, pompano, bluefish, black drum and some flounder; Surf City reports good-sized Spanish on Gotcha plugs, slot and occasional over-slot reds, and evening mixed bags of black drum, mullet, spots and croakers; Seaview reports the mullet migration starting, Spanish and blues on Gotchas or diamond jigs, and flounder, sea mullet, spot, sheepshead, trout and black drum on the bottom. Coastal Angler's Topsail column is still Sept. 1, 34 days old. Setups grounded in that and today's tide: at first light the sound is two hours into the ebb, so a Spook Jr. or another walking topwater over the grass edges and the dock shadows for the trout now that the water has dropped a degree, then as the ebb bottoms out from 8:15 to 10:27, cut mullet or a live finger mullet on a Carolina rig fan-cast a couple of feet off the creek-mouth channel edges for the drum; on the long flood from 10:27 to 4:34, a Carolina-rigged live finger mullet, the bait the whole report is built on, along the grass edges and across the oyster bars as they cover, or a Gulp shrimp or a three-inch paddletail under a popping cork drifted along the edges, and a Gotcha plug or small metal jig inside the inlet if the Spanish come in on the high as Spring Tide describes; at the 4:34 p.m. high, the day's highest water, a weedless gold spoon or a soft plastic on a weighted swimbait hook in the flooded grass and under the docks; on the evening ebb, Carolina-rigged cut mullet again off the channel edges for the drum in the dark, and a half-ounce bucktail with a Gulp trailer bounced through the channel edges for the flounder, which are release only; after dark, the dock lights on the night flood with a small white paddletail or a live shrimp under a float for the trout, and a fiddler crab or a piece of shrimp tight to the bridge pilings and oyster rocks on the moving water for the black drum and sheepshead. On the beach, Gotcha plugs and small metal or epoxy jigs for the Spanish on the flooding surf from the 9:10 a.m. low toward the 3:52 p.m. high, under the birds, with the glass minnows the shops describe as the forage and false albacore mixed in outside the inlet. The reference calendar lists October as the spot run's peak, with red drum, trout, bluefish, sea mullet and black drum, and the piers' spot and croaker lines fit it.
