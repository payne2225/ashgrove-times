  • Williams River — 56.2 cfs, 1.35 feet at 5:15 a.m., steady on both against 56.2 cfs and 1.35 feet in the fetcher's comparison. 56 cfs - prime wading water. Against Thursday's edition reading of 54.9 cfs and 1.34 feet at 5:15 a.m. the gauge is up 1.3 cfs and 0.01 feet on the day, the first morning in 20 that is not down, after 19 straight falling mornings since the Oct. 17 crest. It is not a rise: for 48 hours the USGS trace has flickered between 54.9 and 56.2 cfs, one gauge increment, with the stage at 1.34 or 1.35, and 5:15 a.m. landed on the upper step, which has held since midnight. The daily means say where the recession went: 1,360 cfs on Oct. 15, 653 on Oct. 17, 141 on Oct. 24, 70.7 on Oct. 31, 65.0 on Nov. 1, 60.8, 57.7, 55.5 and 55.6 through Thursday, flattened to the gauge's resolution, with no rain signal anywhere on the trace. The read string is the fetcher's own verdict, printed as it stands, "56 cfs - prime wading water." Stocking: WVDNR's fish-stocking page, fetched this morning, carries an empty list under the waters stocked, and no November report names the Williams, the Cranberry or the Summersville tailwater; the river's most recent stocking this paper has reported is still Oct. 29. At 56 cfs and clear the Williams is as low as it has been since the Oct. 15 freshet and fishes as the file's low-water problem: pocket water and plunge pools shrunk to their cores, every fish in the deepest slot of each pocket, the water cold and glass-clear, and a wading angler the loudest thing on the river. Structure is pocket water, plunge pools and undercut banks; forage is caddis, stoneflies and sculpin. So: a long leader, nine to 12 feet to 6X or 7X, and small flies, a size 16 to 18 caddis pupa or a size 18 soft-hackle swung into the plunge-pool tailouts, a size 16 to 18 pheasant tail or small black stonefly nymph under a tiny indicator or a dry-dropper through the pocket seams, a size 20 to 22 blue-winged olive on top through the middle of an overcast day, and for the one bigger fish in each plunge pool a size 8 to 10 olive or black sculpin pattern fished on a short line into the foam at the head of the pool at first or last light, before the sun gets on it; an egg pattern or a San Juan worm under a small indicator still covers the holdover stocked pools. The heavy-nymph-on-the-seam game is for 500 cfs, and the trace shows nothing like it coming.USGS 03186500; WVDNR
