On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 171 cfs and rising, 1.97 feet. 171 cfs - prime wading water. Up more than threefold from 53.7 cfs a day earlier, the biggest jump of the summer, after Saturday-morning thunderstorms drew a severe thunderstorm warning for Richwood, Camden-on-Gauley, Craigsville and the Cranberry area at the river's headwaters (NWS Charleston); the paper has no measured basin total. At 171 and carrying color this is a nymph-on-the-seam river, not a long-leader one: a heavy stonefly nymph or a weighted sculpin swung slow through the plunge pools and along the seams where the pocket water dumps in, with caddis, stoneflies and sculpin the forage. The front has passed and NWS Charleston's 2 a.m. discussion has dry weather through Wednesday, so the drop should be quick.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.61 feet and rising. 25.6 ft and rising - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Up from 25.04 feet a day ago and now about 0.7 feet above the National Weather Service forecast issued Saturday morning, which had the pool easing to 24.7 feet by Tuesday before the rain worked downstream. A little more push and a little more color on the pool, so the wing-dam tips and riprap still fish - shad are the forage, a white swimbait or a spoon over the tips at first light - with a football jig dragged into the slack behind the tips once the sun is up.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.87 feet and rising. 26.9 ft and rising - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Up almost a foot from 25.9 feet a day ago and above the Weather Service's Saturday-morning forecast, which had the gauge back to 26.2 feet by this morning and 25.7 by Tuesday. There is current on the lock wall and in the barge cuts again: a crawfish-colored jig pitched into the slack behind the cuts and a skipjack-pattern swimbait along the riprap where the flow pins bait against it.USGS 03206000

North Carolina

  • Topsail Sound — Sound highs 4:07 AM at 3.4 ft and 4:51 PM at 4.5 ft at Hampstead; lows 10:37 AM at 0.2 ft and 11:43 PM at 0.7 ft. Fish the moving water either side of high at 4:07 AM and 4:51 PM in the sound. The Fisherman's Post September report, published Tuesday, Sept. 1 and five days old, has red drum steady at docks and creek mouths on early topwater, flounder "abundant" with quality 20-to-24-inch fish on a 1-ounce bucktail and Gulp trailer, bottom fishing off the Jolly Roger producing red drum, black drum, Virginia mullet, trout and the odd pompano, and black drum, pompano and croakers off the Surf City pier with Gotcha plugs taking Spanish; kings are still thin, and the shops expect them in 50-foot-plus water soon. Coastal Angler's Sept. 1 column adds black drum on cut shrimp at the bridges and oyster rocks. Flounder want moving water on structure, so work the flood into the 4:51 PM high; reds want the last of the fall, into tonight's 11:43 PM low. Waning crescent at 30%, so neap tides and a gentle inlet. Water 83.8F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
