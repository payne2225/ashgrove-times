Around the Leagues
Hoshoryu returns to the dohyo, calls the knee 'much better,' decides by Friday

Yokozuna (grand champion) Hoshoryu practiced Tuesday at Tatsunami stable in Tokyo, offering his chest to lower-ranked wrestlers without a brace on his surgically repaired right knee: "It's gotten much better and there's strength in the leg." He took no bouts at Friday's Yokozuna Deliberation Council session, and swelling the next day forced a rest day. He will decide whether to enter the Aki basho (autumn tournament), which opens Sunday at Ryogoku Kokugikan, by the morning of Friday's bout-drawing meeting; he has missed two straight tournaments.

  • Williams River — 71 cfs, 1.46 feet, falling. 71 cfs - prime wading water. Down from Sunday morning's post-storm reading of 166 cfs and back to low, clear late-summer water. At 71 this is a long-leader, small-fly river: a size 16 caddis or a small stonefly nymph fished through the pocket water and plunge pools, with caddis, stoneflies and sculpin the forage, and a small streamer along the undercut banks first thing. NWS Charleston's 5:17 a.m. discussion has warm, placid weather through Wednesday afternoon, then a front bringing showers and storms Wednesday night into early Friday with heavy downpours and a localized flash-flood concern, so the window is today and Wednesday.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 25.0 feet and falling. 25.0 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Easing a few tenths a day since the weekend rise. Normal pool is structure fishing: shad are the forage, so a white swimbait or a spoon over the wing-dam tips at first light, then a football jig dragged into the slack behind the tips once the sun is on the water.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.3 feet and falling. 26.3 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. The Weather Service's forecast for the Huntington gauge, read at 5 a.m., has it easing to 25.9 feet by 8 a.m. with no flooding. A little current still runs on the lock wall and in the barge cuts: a crawfish-colored jig pitched into the slack behind the cuts, and a skipjack-pattern swimbait along the riprap where the flow pins bait against it. The Corps' Huntington District site was unreachable again, so no lock notice is printed.USGS 03206000
