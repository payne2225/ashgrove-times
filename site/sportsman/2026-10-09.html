What 55 cfs means: it is the lowest number of the whole run down from the Sept. 23 crest of 1,450 cfs, 16 days of recession, the lowest since the 56.2 of Sept. 18, and within a few cfs of the 47.6 and 44.2 the river touched Sept. 10-11 before the September rains, so this is late-summer base flow in the second week of October, the pocket water reduced to the plunge-pool heads and the undercuts, and the fish concentrated and spooky. The Weather Service's active list for West Virginia carried no product at 5:50 a.m., no frost advisory for Pocahontas, where the river rises, and no fog statement. At 55 cfs and clear, the river is a long-leader, small-fly problem and nothing else: a 12-foot leader to 6X or 7X, a size 18 or 20 Blue-Winged Olive or Parachute Adams over the tailouts once the sun is on the water and the olives start, a size 18 Pheasant Tail or Zebra Midge on a short dropper under the dry through the plunge-pool heads, where the fish hold in the only depth left, a size 16 soft hackle swung through the tailouts midday, and a size 12 or 14 October Caddis dry alone over the undercut banks in the last hour of light, the one big bite on the river; with the stonefly nymphs under the rocks rather than on the water in October, a small black stonefly nymph on the dropper is the other choice through the plunge pools. Approach from downstream, stay on the gravel, and make the first cast count, because at this flow every fish in a pool sees the wade; a small olive sculpin stripped tight to the undercuts is a last-light bet only on water this thin.
