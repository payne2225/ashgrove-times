On the Water

West Virginia

  • Williams River — 103 cfs, 1.65 feet, receding. 103 cfs - prime wading water. The gauge's own series shows the river crested late Saturday night after the storms and has been dropping since, roughly halving from Sunday morning to this morning's 103 cfs; the fetcher's trend flag reads rising against a pre-storm baseline and is not the story. At 103 and clearing this is back to a light-tippet, small-fly river: a size 16 caddis or a small stonefly nymph on a long leader through the pocket water, with caddis, stoneflies and sculpin the forage. NWS Charleston's 5:19 a.m. discussion has quieter weather through midweek, then a cold front with showers and storms Wednesday night into Thursday and a marginal risk of excessive rain Thursday, so the window is Monday through Wednesday.USGS 03186500
  • Ohio River at Point Pleasant — 24.9 feet and falling. 24.9 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Down from Sunday's rise and now tracking the National Weather Service forecast issued Sunday morning, which has the pool near this level Tuesday and easing a few tenths through Friday. The push from Saturday's rain is off the pool, so it is structure fishing again - shad are the forage, a white swimbait or a spoon over the wing-dam tips at first light, then a football jig dragged into the slack behind the tips once the sun is up.USGS 03201500
  • Ohio River at Huntington — 26.3 feet and falling. 26.3 ft and falling - normal pool. Ordinary bank access. Falling toward the Weather Service's Sunday-morning forecast, which has the gauge easing through Friday; there is still a little current on the lock wall and in the barge cuts. A crawfish-colored jig pitched into the slack behind the cuts and a skipjack-pattern swimbait along the riprap where the flow pins bait against it. The Corps' Huntington District site was unreachable again, so no lock notice is printed.USGS 03206000

The Fisherman's Post September report, published Tuesday, Sept. 1 and six days old, has East Coast Sports on Spanish mackerel and bluefish on casting jigs with a few tarpon and cobia on live bait at the piers, reds and trout inshore, and the guides calling flounder "all over the place"; the Jolly Roger Pier has Spanish and blues hit-or-miss with red drum, black drum, Virginia mullet, speckled trout and the odd pompano on the bottom, and the Surf City Pier black drum, pompano and croakers, Spanish on Gotcha plugs. Flounder want moving water on structure, so work the flood into the 5:52 PM high; reds want the last of the fall, which runs into tonight's 12:37 AM low. Waning crescent at 21%, so neap tides and a gentle inlet. Water 81.5F at Beaufort, 60 miles up the coast.NOAA CO-OPS 8657813
