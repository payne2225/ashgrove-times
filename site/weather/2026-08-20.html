Thursday, August 20
The swamp air rolls back in — a morning Flood Watch up north, and the real soaker's holding for Friday
By Jim Claudtore
Parkersburg — Pat

The wet corner today. A Flood Watch runs until 2 PM (NWS Charleston) for Wood County and the southeast-Ohio line — rounds of heavy downpours this morning on ground that's been soaking all week, so the creeks and low spots along the northern river are where water shows first. High only 78°F (26°C) under the cloud deck, easing to 64°F (18°C) tonight, and the mugginess is back — afternoon humidity 77% off a 68°F dew point. Watch the low-water crossings this morning, Pat; nothing you can't drive around if you look first.

Apple Grove — Nate & Ian · Huntington — Trav

The swamp returns, but gently — for now. Gray and sticky with a scattered afternoon sprinkle at best (rain chances only 29–32%, nothing organized), afternoon humidity back up into the 60s-to-70s — Apple Grove the stickier at 72% off a 68°F dew point, Huntington a touch drier at 61%. Highs 80°F (27°C) at Apple Grove, 82°F (28°C) at Huntington, muggy lows near 66–68°F (19–20°C). Hurricane runs the same warm, gray setup, so Nate's commute is just a humid one. Don't let today fool you — it's the front porch of Friday's real rain (below).

Summersville — Clayton

The cool, muggy holdout again — high only 75°F (24°C) down to 62°F (17°C), afternoon humidity a heavy 75% off a 67°F dew point. Best rain odds of the crew today at 35%, but still just scattered mountain showers, not the main event. That lands tomorrow: the high country around Summersville and Cowen is Friday's bullseye.

The Hart, Prince George — Kirsten

Ninth straight bluebird, and while our swamp air comes back for seconds, the Hart just declines the invitation: sunny, high 26°C (79°F) on genuinely dry air (afternoon humidity down at 39% off a 50°F dew point), UV 6, the air pristine at AQI 21. Then a clear, cool 10°C (50°F) overnight — windows-wide, no-AC sleeping weather at its best. The kind of run that makes late August up there look unfair.

Honorable mentions
  • Lesage — Justin: muggy and gray as the wet air rolls back — 81°F (27°C) / 67°F (19°C), afternoon humidity climbing back to 60%; and with the rain chasing everyone indoors, the Lesage laundromat has once again removed Justin from the premises, the sign newly taped over the folding table reading only "these are for laundry. all of them. please."
  • Hinton — Greg: New River Gorge country stays quiet today — 80°F (27°C) / 64°F (18°C), humidity 65%, barely a sprinkle. Bank the calm: Hinton's tomorrow's soaked seat, with three-plus inches modeled for Friday.
  • North Bennington — Wes: dry, comfortable, and the coolest night by a mile — 75°F (24°C) / 51°F (11°C) on crisp 43% air. Vermont sitting this one out entirely.
Quick hits
  • Cowen, WV: empty cabin, quiet and mountain-cool today — H 74°F (23°C) / L 61°F (16°C), humidity 74%. Enjoy it dry while it lasts: the Williams River valley sits squarely in Friday's heavy-rain target, better than two inches modeled.
Topsail Beach, NC — beach & inlet report

Still the furnace: a Heat Advisory runs 11 AM–8 PM (NWS Wilmington), heat index climbing toward 109°F on oppressive air. High near 88°F (31°C), barely backing off to a sticky 79°F (26°C) overnight, afternoon humidity 80% off a brutal 80°F dew point. Rain's a no-show (8%), so there's no relief in it — hydrate and find shade through the afternoon. UV 8 — SPF or regret. Air's nudged moderate too (AQI upper-40s now, topping near 70 later on haze).

🌊 Tides: their water — the Hampstead sound gauge — runs an afternoon high at 3:07 PM (3.9 ft), then falls all evening to a 9:45 PM low (1.2 ft); that long fall is the red-drum window, and today it runs well past dark. The ocean/inlet reference (Ocean City Beach pier) leads out front: high 2:22 PM, low 8:55 PM. The sound lags the inlet about 53 minutes on the high but ~65 on the low — it drains slower than it fills, so that evening ebb stretches a little longer than the clock says.

🌙 Moon: first quarter, 47% lit — dead-center neap tides, so the swings stay gentle (today's ocean swing just 3.2 ft) and the inlet current's soft. Easy water, not the funnel it runs on a full moon.

🎣 What's running: (monthly report, published Aug 3 — 17 days old, not a live read.) August is red-drum prime: topwater plugs at first light, then cut menhaden or finger mullet as it heats up — and the reds want the last of the falling tide, so that long afternoon-into-night ebb toward the 9:45 low is your window (headlamp weather, it runs past dark). Speckled trout are a dawn game in the heat — topwater in the first hour after sunrise, paddle tails in the deeper holes later. Surf City Pier's giving up specks, pompano, sea mullet, spots and the odd red drum, plus Spanish over 3 lbs on plugs; Jolly Roger's the trophy end — tarpon to an estimated 110 lbs and a 63-lb cobia on live king rigs off the end. Flounder are thick but stay catch-and-release; the harvest window doesn't open till September.

Confidence: high — today's a muggy, gray, scattered-shower day, not a washout, everywhere but the Parkersburg flood corner. And the honest correction: I billed today as the big 50–60% round, and it slipped a day. The real soaker is Friday — the mountains and southern gorge (Cowen, Hinton, Summersville) are the bullseye at two to nearly four inches modeled, and the river crew gets a good soaking too. Bust case: today's mountain showers over-perform, or Friday's rain jumps the gun late tonight.

This morning & today: that Parkersburg-area Flood Watch is real — heavy downpours on saturated ground through early afternoon, so don't drive water you can't see the bottom of. And on the Carolina coast, the Heat Advisory is the day's other danger: heat index to 109°F, so hydrate and stay out of the worst of it. Trav's porch-and-blanket exemption stands, as ever.

WV sun's down 8:16 at Apple Grove. Air's holding in the good range crew-wide (AQI in the 30s, tipping the low-50s midday) — no smoke, and the returning rain only scrubs it cleaner.
