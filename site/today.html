Vance rules out Iran talks until attacks on shipping stop
Kuwait says it intercepted Iranian missiles and drones; Brent holds near $96 as Hormuz transits lag pre-war.
By Wire Reports

Vice President JD Vance said Thursday the United States will not negotiate with Iran until it stops shooting at commercial shipping, as a U.S. official said Iran's overnight strikes did not affect American facilities in Kuwait or elsewhere in the region. Kuwait's army said it intercepted the missiles and drones, and the United Arab Emirates said no attack on it took place.

Brent crude rose to $95.67 a barrel Friday, up 7.1% on the week, France 24 reported, while Lloyd's List Intelligence counted an average of 102 ship transits a day through the Strait of Hormuz last week, against more than 130 a day before the war, CBS News reported. AAA said U.S. diesel reached a record $5.85 a gallon and regular gasoline averaged $4.14.

Vance said he would not call the six-month conflict a war because "right now, there is no active shooting," and said the U.S. is investigating but is "extremely skeptical" of Iranian reports that a U.S. strike hit a wedding in Kuhestak. South Korea said it is reviewing contributions to Hormuz security, France 24 reported, and Iran's first vice president said any answer to a future U.S. attack would be "asymmetric."
