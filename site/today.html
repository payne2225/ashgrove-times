US orders diplomats out of Saudi Arabia as military thins Mideast posts
Houthi strikes on Saudi airports and Iranian threats drive the drawdown; Dammam airport toll rises to two
By Wire Reports

The State Department ordered non-emergency employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia on Wednesday as the U.S. military quietly reduced its footprint at bases across the Middle East, with Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities continuing and President Donald Trump weighing new action against Iran.

Spokesperson Tommy Pigott said Houthi attacks on civilian airports and infrastructure prompted the order; other embassies remain on "restricted operations" without ordered departures, CBS News reported. A U.S. official and a senior military official told CBS the drawdown answers Houthi and Iranian threats. Central Command said it does not discuss force movements.

Saudi civil aviation authorities said Thursday the toll from Wednesday's attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam rose to two dead and eight injured. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck four tankers in the Gulf and warned the confrontation would "extend to the bases of the aggressors"; Brent crude rose 3.2% to $112.27, CBS reported.
