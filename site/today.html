Trump says deportation drive was 'too tough' after midterm rout
In a 60 Minutes interview, the president gave himself an A+ on the border and said he would attack Iran again
By Wire Reports

President Donald Trump said Sunday that his administration's deportation campaign was "too tough" on long-settled immigrants with jobs, his first concession on the policy since Republicans lost both chambers of Congress on Tuesday. He spoke to CBS News' Ross Douthat in a 60 Minutes interview broadcast Sunday night.

Trump said immigration "both helped and hurt" him at the polls and that he earned "an A+ for closing up the border," but that people working at landscaping companies or hotels and paying taxes were removed too. Exit polling cited by CBS found 58% of voters said the immigration actions had gone too far and 63% disapproved of his performance; Trump said the main reason for the losses was that "I was not on the ballot."

He said he would answer "emphatic yes" if asked whether he would strike Iran again, called impeachment calls against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth "Very unfair," and said he would "love to do something on immigration" with Democrats. On Friday, about an hour after the taping, Trump named Stephen Miller, the deportation policy's architect, to replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff.
