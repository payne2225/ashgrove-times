Trump approves firing squad execution for Fort Hood shooter Nidal Hasan
Pentagon says Hegseth recommended the method; it would be the U.S. military's first execution since 1961
By Wire Reports

The Trump administration has approved executing former Army Maj. Nidal Hasan, who killed 13 people in the 2009 shooting at Fort Hood, Texas, by Army firing squad, chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said Monday night. The Army secretary will set the time and place, Parnell said, adding that "judgement day for Hasan has finally come."

Parnell said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth recommended the method and President Trump approved it. Hasan, a former Army psychiatrist, was convicted at a 2013 court-martial of 13 counts of premeditated murder and 32 of attempted murder and sentenced to death. He represented himself, admitted the shooting and said he acted to protect Muslims from soldiers bound for Afghanistan.

It would be the military's first execution since 1961, when Pvt. John Bennett was hanged, and its first by firing squad since 1945, according to the Death Penalty Information Center. The Supreme Court declined last year to hear Hasan's appeal over the judge closing his courtroom. South Carolina used firing squads in three executions last year, the first in the U.S. since 2010.
