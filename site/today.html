Lula wins fourth term as Brazil's president; Bolsonaro rejects result
Final tally gives Lula 51.8% and a margin of nearly 4.4 million votes; senator seeks to annul the vote
By Wire Reports

Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won a nonconsecutive fourth term Sunday with 51.8% of the runoff vote over right-wing Sen. Flavio Bolsonaro, who refused to concede and said he would seek annulment after polling hours were extended.

The final count gave Lula a margin of nearly 4.4 million votes, The Associated Press reported, reversing the Oct. 4 first round, in which Bolsonaro led 47% to 45%. Justice Cristiano Zanin extended voting by two hours at both campaigns' request, but some polling stations had already shut down their voting machines and could not reopen, and Bolsonaro called the result "inexplicable."

Lula, who turns 81 on Tuesday, takes office Jan. 5 as the first candidate since democracy returned in 1989 to lose a first round and win the runoff. Bolsonaro, 45, had pledged to pardon his father, ex-President Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving 27 years for plotting a coup. Federal police are investigating election-day transport disruptions in five states, Al Jazeera reported, citing Reuters.
