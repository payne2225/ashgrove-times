U.S. strikes Iranian targets on Qeshm Island on the eve of Election Day
Officials call the Hormuz strikes defensive; Tehran's parliament speaker warns of 'a new battlefield'
By Wire Reports

The U.S. military struck Iranian targets on Qeshm Island near the Strait of Hormuz late Monday, three U.S. officials told CBS News, hours before polls opened in the midterm elections. A senior official said the limited strikes answered Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fire at an American aircraft over the strait.

A U.S. official told The Associated Press the strikes hit "Iranians using surface to air missiles against U.S. assets." Iran's state news agency IRNA reported explosions in rapid succession on Qeshm, with witnesses seeing two planes over the island, and Fars reported blasts at Bandar Lengeh. Brent crude, which fell 7.6% to $104.77 Monday on hopes of a deal, rose $1.84 to $106.61 by 7:44 GMT, Al Jazeera reported.

Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Tuesday that any U.S. "strategic error" would open "a new battlefield in the war" and that Iran is at its highest defensive readiness. Vice President JD Vance said Monday, before the strikes, that he and Trump "feel pretty good" about a negotiated settlement; AP reported Qatari mediators plan more talks this week.
