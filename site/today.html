Gunman kills 6 in Krakow's main square; Poland calls it terrorism
Three relatives found dead at the suspect's home; the interior minister said he was drawn to far-right ideology
By Wire Reports

Agunman firing a military-style rifle from a car killed six people and wounded 15 in Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday, Polish officials said, and police later found his parents and sister shot dead at his home about 90 miles to the northeast. The dead were four women and two men, a British national among them; Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the attack terrorism.

The suspect, a Polish national in his mid-20s in military-style clothing, was shot by police in an exchange of fire and taken to a hospital in custody, Krakow police said. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said the man "was fascinated by far-right ideologies" and that more weapons were found in his car. Two officers were wounded, one seriously; the injured include citizens of Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal and Russia.

The National Prosecutor's Office opened a terrorism investigation, AFP reported, and the government declared two days of national mourning from Sunday; Krakow's mayor sealed the square and declared the city in mourning through Wednesday. The U.S. State Department said it knew of no Americans hurt. Mass shootings are rare in Poland.
