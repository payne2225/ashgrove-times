U.S. aircraft disables merchant ship running the Iran blockade
Central Command says it fired on the stern of the Palau-flagged Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman after warnings; the crew was unharmed
By Wire Reports

The U.S. military disabled a merchant ship in the Gulf of Oman that was trying to run its blockade of Iranian ports, U.S. Central Command said Friday. A military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer on Thursday after the crew was given multiple warnings, the command said.

The ship's propulsion was disabled and its civilian crew was unharmed, Central Command said. The vessel had left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas, and ship-tracking sites listed its destination as Singapore. Since the blockade resumed in July, the military says it has redirected 158 compliant ships and disabled six that refused, the last on Nov. 7.

The strike comes as the administration weighs its options in the war with Iran, which began in late February and has settled into a stalemate; Trump said Friday the war will end whenever he wants. Iran's navy commander, Shahram Irani, said on state television Friday that Iran will carry out operations to break the blockade. The Pacific Archer is at least the 15th vessel disabled since spring.
