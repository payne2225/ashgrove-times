Colom beats Hyde-Smith in Mississippi for Democrats' eighth Senate flip
The AP called the race Wednesday after days of absentee and provisional counting; Colom is the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction
By Wire Reports

Democrat Scott Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi on Wednesday after local officials spent several days processing absentee and provisional ballots, the Associated Press and CBS News projected; a few dozen votes separated them the day after the election.

Colom will be Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction and the first Democrat the state has sent to the Senate since 1982; the last Democrat elected to this seat, in 1947, was a segregationist. Trump carried the state by about 20 points in 2024, and national Democrats began spending on the race only last month, after the Cook Political Report moved it to lean Republican.

Hyde-Smith, 67, appointed in 2018 and twice elected over Mike Espy, conceded in a video message Wednesday night, saying she respected the voters' decision. The seat is Democrats' eighth flip after North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas and Maine, giving them at least 55 seats in January with Alaska still undecided, per CBS.
