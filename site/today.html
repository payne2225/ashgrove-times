Trump defends Russia sanctions waiver, says Zelenskyy wants war to go on
Bessent declines three times on NBC to call Ukraine an ally; two Republicans seek a Starlink probe
By Wire Reports

President Donald Trump on Sunday defended his waiver of new sanctions on Russia, writing on Truth Social that he did not want to "drive, foolishly, Russia away from the bargaining table" and that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy "doesn't want to end this war, and probably never did."

Zelenskyy told NBC's "Meet the Press" that the United States siding with Russia would be a "historic mistake" and that Ukraine "will not prevail" if the two align. He said intelligence sharing was blocked for three days and that SpaceX's Starlink limits followed a "message from American government." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, asked three times whether Ukraine is an ally, did not say.

Trump signed the waiver Friday, two days before the sanctions took effect, after announcing a deal Oct. 9 for Russia to ship more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel. Rep. Mike Turner and Sen. Kevin Cramer, both Republicans, called on CBS for a congressional inquiry into the Starlink disruption, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio said no one ordered.
