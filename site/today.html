Abrego Garcia leaves U.S. for Spain, ending two-year deportation fight
Lawyers say he departed Dulles on a commercial flight rather than on an ICE plane to Liberia
By Wire Reports

Kilmar Abrego Garcia, the Salvadoran national whose mistaken deportation to El Salvador last year became a test case for President Donald Trump's immigration crackdown, left the United States for Spain on Tuesday night, his lawyers and Trump administration officials said, ending a legal fight of nearly two years.

Abrego Garcia, 31, boarded a commercial flight from Washington Dulles International Airport to Madrid with his wife and brother present, NBC News reported. A federal judge had ordered him to leave the country by Tuesday, a U.S. official told NBC News. His attorney Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg said he left "a free man" rather than "in shackles" on a government flight to Liberia.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin called the departure a self-deportation. Abrego Garcia was sent to El Salvador's CECOT prison in March 2025, returned in June 2025 to face smuggling charges a judge later dismissed, and fought removal to Liberia. His lawyer said he may seek to return as the husband and father of U.S. citizens.
