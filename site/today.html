Inflation accelerates to 3.7% as gasoline jumps 9.1% in a month
Core prices climb to 2.6%; markets put odds of an Oct. 28 Fed hike above 55% and the Nasdaq falls 1.8%
By Wire Reports

U.S. consumer prices rose 3.7% in the 12 months through September, up from 3.4% in each of the two prior months and above the 3.6% economists expected, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said Wednesday, with a 9.1% one-month jump in gasoline accounting for more than half of the increase.

Prices rose 0.6% from August, and core inflation, which excludes food and energy, climbed to 2.6% from 2.4%, above the 2.5% economists had forecast. Energy was up 20.4% from a year earlier and airline fares 22.4%, CBS News reported. Regular gasoline averaged $4.33 a gallon Wednesday, up from $2.98 before the Iran war began, according to AAA.

The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate to 3.75% to 4% in September, its first increase since 2023, and meets next Oct. 27-28. NBC News reported market odds of an October hike rose above 55% after the release; the Nasdaq fell 1.8% and the S&P 500 1.1%. White House economic adviser Kevin Hassett called the last report before the Nov. 3 midterms "a disappointing number."
