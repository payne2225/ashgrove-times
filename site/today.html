Rescue resumes on Nepal-Tibet border as flood toll passes 630
Nearly 3,000 people are missing after crews paused for a day while an unstable barrier lake overflowed
By Wire Reports

Rescue crews in Nepal and Tibet went back to work Saturday after pausing while a newly formed barrier lake at the disaster site overflowed, with Nepal's police counting 626 dead and Chinese authorities at least seven more across the border. Nepal's disaster authority lists 2,426 people missing on its side, 517 of them foreign tourists and workers, and Chinese officials report 554 missing in Tibet.

The disaster began Wednesday morning when a glacier collapsed at roughly 5,200 meters and plunged 1,200 meters into the Lende River valley, sending a surge that raised the Trishuli River nine meters in half an hour through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts and Tibet's Gyirong county. The U.S. State Department has said about 90 Americans were impacted, many of them pilgrims bound for Mount Kailash; no American deaths have been confirmed.

Glacial scientist Alton Byers said the debris dam holding back the new lake is highly unstable and that the safest outcome would be a natural, gradual drainage. Aid has arrived from India, the U.N., the Red Cross federation, Australia, South Korea and the United States, and Nepal has asked for specialist help including tunnel rescue teams.
