Alberta votes 76% to stay in Canada, rejecting a separation path
Smith promises no second vote this mandate; separatists say they will test her hold on the UCP
By Wire Reports

Albertans voted by a wide margin Monday to remain in Canada, with early returns showing about 76% rejecting a path toward a binding separation referendum, Global News and CTV News reported as Elections Alberta counted ballots overnight in the nonbinding vote called by Premier Danielle Smith's government.

CTV News projected 76.4% for remaining and 23.6% for beginning the legal process toward a separation vote, with the remain side passing 1 million votes shortly before 1 a.m. MT. More than 1.35 million ballots were cast in advance polls and by mail, Global News reported, against about 810,000 cast that way in the 2023 provincial election.

Smith, who said she voted to remain, had promised that 50% plus one would carry the day and that there would be no follow-up referendum in this mandate; she addresses the province Tuesday at 6:45 p.m. MT. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Albertans "chose unity." Results on nine other questions, on immigration and the Constitution, are expected Wednesday.
