Mexico captures cartel boss 'El Chapo Isidro' as 11 die in Sinaloa fighting
Fausto Isidro Meza Flores was on the FBI's 10 most wanted list; four soldiers and seven alleged gunmen were killed in Guasave
By Wire Reports

Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, the cartel leader known as "El Chapo Isidro" and one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives, in an overnight operation in Guasave, Sinaloa, that left at least 11 people dead, Mexico's Defense Ministry said Saturday.

Seven alleged cartel gunmen and four soldiers were killed, the ministry said. The firefight began about 7 p.m. Friday and spread across the city, with gunmen burning vehicles at roadblocks and helicopters firing overhead, The Associated Press reported; troops did not extract Meza Flores until about 4 a.m. Saturday. He was flown to Mexico City with two lieutenants, and five other suspects were detained.

Meza Flores led the Beltran Leyva cartel before founding his own group, an ally of the Sinaloa Cartel's "Los Mayos" faction, and the United States had offered a $5 million reward for his arrest. Mexico said U.S. intelligence helped the operation. Security Secretary Omar Garcia Harfuch said troops would stay in Sinaloa, calling the arrests "an important step in pacifying Sinaloa."
