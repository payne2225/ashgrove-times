Democrats win House and Senate, ending Republican control of Congress
Seven GOP Senate seats flipped, Maine, Texas and Kansas among them; Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled
By Wire Reports

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, NBC News and CBS News projected late Tuesday, handing President Trump a Congress run by the opposition party for the final two years of his term. CBS News called the Senate for Democrats at 11:44 p.m. ET.

Democrats flipped seven Republican-held Senate seats — North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and Maine, where Sen. Susan Collins conceded — and held Michigan and Georgia, CBS News reported. Alaska and Mississippi were uncalled as of 4:20 a.m. ET, with about 75 House races undetermined. CBS exit polls put Trump's approval at 38%.

Trump congratulated Democrats in a call with CBS News and said he has "total veto power." House Democrats hold leadership elections the week of Nov. 16, and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries needs 218 votes Jan. 3 to become speaker, NBC News reported. Sen. Chuck Schumer is expected to return as majority leader when the new Congress convenes in January.
