Trump names Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Wiles
Wiles resigned Thursday night after the midterm losses; Miller, architect of the deportation program, starts Monday
By Wire Reports

President Trump named Stephen Miller White House chief of staff on Friday, replacing Susie Wiles, who ran his 2024 campaign and had held the job since his return to office, three days after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress. Miller, 41, starts Monday, Nov. 9.

Wiles submitted her resignation Thursday night, a U.S. official and a senior White House adviser told CBS News, and three sources tied it to the midterm losses. Trump wrote on Truth Social that he "wanted to make a change" and that Wiles moves to the Trump Political Operation. Miller, the deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser, is the architect of the administration's deportation program.

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Trump "learned nothing" from the defeat, and Rep. Robert Garcia, in line to chair House Oversight, said Miller "should lawyer up." The shakeup reached the Justice Department, where John Eisenberg, head of the National Security Division, and Jason Reding Quinones, the U.S. attorney in South Florida, resigned Friday, a day after Civil Rights chief Harmeet Dhillon.
