Glacial flood kills at least 160 in Nepal, hundreds missing
An ice avalanche dammed and released a Himalayan river; the Trishuli rose 27 feet in half an hour
By Wire Reports

Flash floods tore down the Trishuli river valley on the Nepal-China border Wednesday morning after a glacier collapse sent ice and rock into the Lhende Khola tributary, killing at least 160 people and burying villages in Nepal's Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts in mud. Officials in Nepal reported 403 people missing, among them 341 foreign nationals, and Chinese state media reported 558 missing across the border in Tibet's Gyirong County.

The missing include 133 Indian pilgrims bound for Mount Kailash, 47 Americans and 33 Britons, Nepal's tourism board said. At least 19 bridges and some 25 miles of road were swept away, along with several hydropower projects. The International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development said the Trishuli rose 9 meters — about 27 feet — within half an hour, a speed and magnitude it called exceptional.

Nepal has asked both India and China to help with the rescue, government spokesperson Sasmit Pokharel said, and U.N. teams deployed to what the organization called a massive humanitarian need. It is the second flood on the Lhende Khola in 14 months, a pattern climate specialists tied to increasingly unstable glaciers in a warming Himalaya.
