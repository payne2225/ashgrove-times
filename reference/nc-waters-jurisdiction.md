# North Carolina: which agency governs the water you are writing about

**This is the thing to get right before printing any NC size or creel
limit.** North Carolina splits its coastal waters between two agencies with
different rules, and the crew fishes on the side that is *not* covered by
the book most people reach for.

## The split, in the state's own words

From the *NC Inland Fishing, Hunting & Trapping Regulations Digest
2026–2027*, page 22 (PDF page 24):

> Both the Wildlife Resources Commission and the Division of Marine
> Fisheries have licensing, management and regulatory authority in certain
> waters along the coast of North Carolina. These waters are designated as
> **inland, joint and coastal** waters. The Wildlife Resources Commission
> has jurisdiction in inland waters, and the **Division of Marine Fisheries
> has jurisdiction (except that pertaining to inland game fishes) in
> coastal waters.** Both agencies have licensing and regulatory authority
> in joint waters.

| Waters | Agency | Reachable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Inland** | NC Wildlife Resources Commission (`ncwildlife.gov`) | Yes |
| **Joint** | both, for licensing and regulation | — |
| **Coastal** | **NC Division of Marine Fisheries** (`deq.nc.gov`, DMF) | **Yes** — verified 2026-08-14 |

## Where the crew fishes, and what that means

**Topsail Sound, about two nautical miles north of New Topsail Inlet.**
That is **coastal water**, so the species they actually target — red drum,
speckled trout, flounder, bluefish, sheepshead — are governed by the
**Division of Marine Fisheries**, not by NCWRC.

**The NCWRC inland digest does not contain their limits.** Its fishing
sections are mountain trout, warmwater game fish and nongame species. Do
not quote an inland creel limit at a saltwater fish; they are different
rules from a different agency.

## So how NC is handled

**Look it up live.** `deq.nc.gov` answers, which is the whole difference
from West Virginia — WVDNR's expired certificate is why WV needed a
transcribed reference file and NC does not. Search DMF for the current
size and creel limits on the day you print them, cite **NCDMF**, and link
the page.

Recreational saltwater fishing in NC also requires a **Coastal Recreational
Fishing Licence (CRFL)**, which is a different licence from the inland one.
Worth a line if the section ever covers licensing.

## If you want a transcribed NC file anyway

The equivalent of the WV pamphlet is the DMF recreational size and bag
limit guide, not the NCWRC digest. That is the document to capture if NC
should ever be pinned the way West Virginia is — but as long as DMF stays
reachable, live lookup is better, because it cannot go stale in the repo.
